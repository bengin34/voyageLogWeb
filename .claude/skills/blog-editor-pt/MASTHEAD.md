# Masthead lens: Brazilian Portuguese desk (pt)

This file is the quality bar for the pt desk, as described in `.claude/skills/blog-editor-core/REFERENCE.md`, section 10. It summarises how the most respected Brazilian travel and consumer publications check their own work, then turns that into questions you ask of every VoyageLog article. It is a standard, never a source: nothing here goes into an article.

Researched on 24 October 2026. Limits of this research, so the owner knows what to re-check:
- The web search budget for the session was exhausted, so pages were reached by direct address only.
- `folha.uol.com.br` (all subdomains) and `abril.com.br` (Viagem e Turismo, Grupo Abril) could not be fetched from this environment. For those two, facts come from the Portuguese Wikipedia articles (secondary sources), and everything about their practice is marked **[inferred]**: it comes from familiarity with their published work, not from a stated policy read this session.
- Melhores Destinos and Proteste were read on their own pages and are marked **[stated]** where a page says it.
- None of the four publishes an AI-content policy that could be found this session. Treat any claim that one exists as unverified.

## 1. The benchmarks

### Folha de S.Paulo, caderno Turismo

**Why it is the benchmark.** Folha set the template for written accountability in Brazilian journalism: it was the first Brazilian outlet to publish its newsroom manual (1984), the first to appoint an ombudsman (1989), and it has run a daily corrections column, “Erramos”, since 1991. Its weekly Turismo section (Thursdays) is the reference for sober, service-led travel journalism in the country.

**Editorial QC approach, in our words.**
- The Manual da Redação (fifth edition, 2018) is presented as a set of norms and commitments, not just a style guide: accuracy, non-partisan and pluralist coverage, and a critical stance towards its own subject. [stated, via Wikipedia]
- Errors are corrected in public, in a fixed place, in a fixed form: what was wrong, and what is right. The correction does not argue or soften. [stated that Erramos exists, via Wikipedia; the format is inferred]
- An ombudsman criticises the paper in its own pages and answers readers. The lesson for us: the editor reads as the sceptical reader, not as the writer's ally. [stated, via Wikipedia]
- Travel pieces disclose at the end when a trip was paid for by a tourism board, airline or hotel, in one plain line. [inferred]
- Practical information is fenced off from the narrative and stated plainly: how to get there, what it costs, when to go. Prices are given in the currency the reader will pay in, often with the moment they were checked. [inferred]
- The house style favours explanation over assumption (a term the reader may not know is explained once) and facts over adjectives. [inferred]

### Viagem e Turismo (Editora Abril)

**Why it is the benchmark.** Launched in November 1995, it describes itself as the first Brazilian publication dedicated only to tourism. The print magazine stopped in 2018; the brand continues as a website and remains the name most Brazilians associate with travel journalism, especially destination guides and “how much it costs” service pieces.

**Editorial QC approach, in our words.** No editorial policy page could be read this session; all of this is [inferred] from its published work.
- Service pieces are organised around the reader's decisions (when to go, where to stay, how to get around, how much to budget), each answered concretely before any colour.
- Lists have an implicit selection logic (by budget, by traveller type, by season), and the logic is visible in the headings rather than claimed as an absolute ranking.
- Prices and opening details are written so the reader can tell they are a snapshot, and older guides are updated rather than left to rot.
- It is aimed at the Brazilian traveller specifically: departures from Brazil, prices in reais, the visa or entry question a Brazilian will ask.

**Pages to re-check:**
- Wikipedia summary used: https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viagem_e_Turismo
- Site: https://viagemeturismo.abril.com.br/ (look for an expediente or princípios editoriais page; Grupo Abril has historically published editorial principles, not confirmed this session)

### Melhores Destinos

**Why it is the benchmark.** Founded in 2008, it is the largest independent Brazilian site for travel deals, miles and destination guides, and the one Brazilians check before buying a flight. It is the best local model for practical information that expires fast and must be right on the day it is published.

**Editorial QC approach, in our words.** [stated] unless marked.
- It calls itself a journalistic site, not an agency: it sells no flights, hotels or packages.
- The core filter: if a piece of information does not help the reader save money, travel better or decide better, it probably does not belong.
- Before publishing a deal, the team checks prices, dates, rules and conditions as they stand at that moment, going to the airline, hotel or airport directly whenever possible. It promises to publish only real offers.
- In news, “informação antes de opinião”: facts are separated from expectations, rumours and advertising.
- It says it runs no paid posts or publieditorial, and that partners cannot buy a positive review. It openly states that it earns affiliate commissions and advertising revenue, and that these do not decide coverage.
- In guides and reviews, the team's own experience (flights, stays, visits) feeds the assessment.
- When information changes, it aims to update quickly. There is no formal corrections policy or change log on the about page.
- Every post carries a visible publication time, and many URLs carry the date. [observed on the home page]

**Pages used:**
- https://www.melhoresdestinos.com.br/sobre-o-melhores-destinos
- https://www.melhoresdestinos.com.br/equipe (named editors per beat; no fact-checking role listed)
- https://www.melhoresdestinos.com.br/anuncie (banner formats; one line claiming independent opinion, no labelling rules)

### Proteste (product and service comparisons)

**Why it is the benchmark.** The largest Brazilian consumer association, non-profit and member-funded, part of Euroconsumers and a member of the international ICRT testing network. It is the local reference for “which one should I pick” comparisons, which is what our app articles are.

**Editorial QC approach, in our words.** [stated] unless marked.
- Independent of governments and companies, non-partisan, with independence listed as a core value.
- Samples are bought anonymously in shops, so what is tested is what the consumer can buy.
- Tests compare products against applicable standards and order them on a quality scale, with the aim of naming the best quality-for-price option.
- Testing is done by qualified specialists; comparative research is shared through the international network.
- Results are presented as a ranking against declared criteria, with a best overall and a best-value pick. [inferred from published tests; the criteria and weights per test are not on the pages read]

**Pages used:**

## 2. What transfers to us, and what does not

| Their practice | Our version |
|---|---|
| Checked against the primary source on publication day (Melhores Destinos) | Every competitor, price and place claim verified today per reference section 5, recorded with its URL in `docs/editorial/pt.md`. The source stays in the report, never in the article. |
| Public corrections, Erramos (Folha) | We have no correction column. Every claim must be one we could correct in one line if a reader showed it was wrong, and the report makes the source traceable. |
| Press-trip disclosure (Folha), affiliate disclosure (Melhores Destinos) | One plain sentence near the top: we make VoyageLog. We have no affiliate links; never imply any. |
| Anonymous purchase and lab tests (Proteste); team's own trips (Melhores Destinos) | We do neither. We read each app's own pages and its Brazilian store listing, and say exactly that when method matters. Never “testamos”, never a trip we did not take. |
| Declared criteria, then ranking (Proteste) | State what the comparison looks at before judging, and apply the same criteria to VoyageLog. |
| Snapshot prices and dates (Viagem e Turismo, Folha) | Prefer wording that does not expire (“gratuito, com plano pago”). If a figure is essential, it is from the Brazilian storefront today and `updated` is set. |
| Named writer, ombudsman | Plural byline, “nós”. The editor plays the ombudsman's role in the pass below. |

## 3. Editor's lens: the questions for every pt article

Run this as a separate read after the line edit (and the house-style and smell-list passes). Each question is pass or fix; fix in place, then note anything significant under “What changed” in the report.

1. **Utility.** Does every section help the reader save, travel better or decide better? A section that does none of the three gets cut or tightened, as long as the article stays above the length minimum.
2. **Fact before opinion.** In comparison paragraphs, is the checkable fact stated first and our judgement after it, clearly as ours (“para quem quer só um diário, isso pesa menos”)?
3. **Checked today.** Is every competitor feature, free tier, account rule, platform, price, event date and place detail in the report with a primary-source URL from today? If not, it is generalised or gone.
4. **Two pages agree.** For any claim that could make a competitor look bad, did the app's own site and its Brazilian store page say the same thing? If they disagree or one is silent, write the safe general version.
5. **Erramos test.** Pick the three most specific claims in the article. If a reader wrote in tomorrow saying one is wrong, could we answer from the report in one line? If not, the claim is not ready.
6. **Will it expire?** Does anything here go stale on a date (prices, “novo”, “este ano”, a free-tier limit, an entry rule)? Rephrase it to stay true, or keep it only if verified today and `updated` is set.
7. **Disclosure.** In comparison and alternatives pieces, is “nós fazemos o VoyageLog” one plain sentence near the top, before the first judgement, and not repeated as a sales line?
8. **Same yardstick.** Are VoyageLog's paid tier, limits and missing features described with the same sobriety as competitors' paid plans? Does VoyageLog visibly lose somewhere it really loses (route tracking, sharing, sync)?
9. **Declared criteria.** Does a comparison say what it compares (privacy, offline, account, export, price model, Portuguese interface) before it ranks or recommends? Does each app in a list have a stated reason to be there, and is the order explained or clearly not a ranking?
10. **Method honesty.** Does any sentence imply testing, trips or usage we did not do (“testamos”, “usamos durante a viagem”, “na prática, o app…” about a competitor)? Replace with what we did: read the app's pages and store listing.
11. **For whom, and for whom not.** Does each recommendation name the reader it suits, and is there a specific “when VoyageLog is the wrong choice” section that sends route-tracking readers to a tracker?
12. **Brazilian reality.** Android named where it matters, prices only in reais from the Brazilian storefront, no service unavailable or irrelevant in Brazil, Portuguese interface only if the Brazilian store listing confirms it.
13. **Explained once.** Would a first-time reader understand JSON, backup, exportar, eSIM or best-of without searching? Each technical term gets one plain explanation the first time, and none after.
14. **Adjectives earn their place.** Every “melhor”, “completo”, “fácil”, “ideal” either has a fact beside it that proves it or is cut. Superlatives about VoyageLog are cut unless `en.js` supports them.
15. **Service completeness.** In how-to sections: what you need first, the steps in order, what it costs (or that it is free), and what can go wrong. Missing any of these is a gap to fill from `en.js`, not from imagination.
16. **Seasons and places.** Is every season, festival month or opening window phrased as the typical window the official source gives, never as a promised date, and verified on that official source?
17. **Commerce kept apart.** Is `{{cta}}` the only place that asks for the install? Does the body describe Pro the way it describes a competitor's paid plan, without urgency or sales language?
18. **People, not a generator.** Does any passage read as filler that could sit in any article on any app? Is every example specific enough that only someone who thought about Brazilian travellers would write it, and still framed as an illustration rather than our own anecdote?
19. **Plural byline.** Is the voice “nós” throughout, with no “eu”, no invented personal trip, and no named author?

## 4. What we never borrow

- Their text, in any length beyond a few words, and their headlines, deks, subheadings or captions.
- Their list selections and rankings: which destinations, apps or products they picked, their order, their “top 10”, their “melhor do teste” or best-value picks.
- Their signature formats and section names (for example a branded guide structure, a deal-alert layout, a recurring column title, the corrections column name) and their slogans.
- Their recognisable phrasing, including the short phrases quoted in this file. Those are here so you understand the standard, not to be echoed.
- Their facts as a shortcut: a price, date or opening rule seen in one of their pieces is not verified until the primary source confirms it.
- Their names. Never mention, quote, link or allude to Folha, Viagem e Turismo, Melhores Destinos, Proteste or any other publication in an article, its FAQ or its frontmatter.
- Their credentials. We do not claim lab tests, anonymous purchases, reporting trips, an ombudsman or a corrections column we do not have.
