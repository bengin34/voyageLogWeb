# Masthead lens: Japanese desk

A quality bar, not a source (reference, section 10). It describes how three publications that Japanese readers trust check their own work, and turns that into questions for our edit. Nothing here is content: never reuse their wording, selections or formats, and never mention them in an article.

Research date: 2026-10-24. Each claim is marked **[stated]** (the publisher says it on its own page, URL given) or **[inferred]** (read from their published work or from third-party descriptions, not from a stated policy). Pages listed under "not yet read" should be checked by the owner before anything here is treated as settled.

## Why these three

| Benchmark | What it stands for in this market | What we take from it |
|---|---|---|
| 『地球の歩き方』 | The default overseas guidebook for Japanese travellers since the late 1970s, built on staff reporting plus reader tips | Dating of practical information, honest disclaimers about change, the habit of separating what was checked from what readers reported |
| 『るるぶ』 (JTBパブリッシング) | The best-selling domestic guide brand; its listing data is what readers expect a service piece to get right | Precision of service data, a public correction trail, a stated data date |
| 『暮しの手帖』 | The ad-free magazine whose 商品テスト defined honest product comparison in Japan | Independence, equal criteria, naming the weak points, the consumer's viewpoint |

『Discover Japan』 stays in SKILL.md as the tone reference for place detail; it is not a QC benchmark here. 『TRANSIT』 and 『ことりっぷ』 were candidates but were not researched in this pass (search budget ran out); add them later if useful.

## 『地球の歩き方』

**Why it is the benchmark.** For overseas travel, it is the book Japanese readers carry and the brand they search for. Its authority rests on two things readers know: staff and contract writers 取材 the destination, and readers send corrections and tips that feed the next edition.

**Editorial QC, in our words.**
- **[stated]** The web edition says it tries to publish the latest and most accurate information, but warns that rules and procedures can change after reporting or after publication, that readings of the same rule can differ, and that the reader uses the information at their own risk. It also disclaims responsibility for linked external sites. (Seen in search excerpts of arukikata.co.jp pages, most likely https://www.arukikata.co.jp/news/257671/ ; the page itself was not opened. The site's general terms are at https://www.arukikata.co.jp/terms/ , not yet read.)
- **[stated]** Its popular-destination ranking is built mainly from reader submissions, with points added for places its own staff reported on, and it says plainly that the ranking is its own method. (Search excerpt of https://www.arukikata.co.jp/news/257671/)
- **[stated]** A public submission form for guidebook tips exists (https://www.arukikata.co.jp/@toukou ; the form's instructions did not load, so how tips are verified is not confirmed). A revision-schedule page lists new and revised editions (https://www.arukikata.co.jp/news/schedule/ , not yet read in full).
- **[stated]** The site's community guideline (https://www.arukikata.co.jp/guideline/) is a posting policy for members, not an editorial code: posts must be the member's own work, copying others is banned, and commercial promotion needs a prior contract. No public editorial-standards, AI or corrections policy was found on the site's navigation.
- **[stated]** Authors are named on author pages (https://www.arukikata.co.jp/author/).
- **[inferred]** Printed guides state when their data was gathered and tell readers to confirm prices, hours and entry rules at the official source before going. Reader tips appear as clearly separate items attributed to the reader, so the book never presents a reader's experience as its own reporting.

**What this means for us.** Separate what we checked from what we did not. Treat change as normal and say so where the reader would act on a fact. Never present someone else's experience, or an imagined one, as our reporting.

## 『るるぶ』

**Why it is the benchmark.** The domestic guide most Japanese readers have held; its pages taught readers to expect address, hours, closing days and price for every listing, and to trust them.

**Editorial QC, in our words.**
- **[stated]** JTBパブリッシング keeps a public trail of errors: its books page combines the release list, the next-edition schedule and 訂正情報 (https://jtbpublishing.co.jp/book/), and individual notices titled お詫び or お詫びと訂正 are posted as news (for example https://jtbpublishing.co.jp/news/IF000121 and https://jtbpublishing.co.jp/news/IF000120 ; notice bodies not read).
- **[stated, via a library catalogue]** A Kyoto edition published in early 2021 carries a data date of 「2020年11月現在」, i.e. the book states when its data was fixed, months before release. (https://www.library.city.uenohara.yamanashi.jp/Libsearch/English/Materials/Detail/168743)
- **[stated]** No public editorial-policy or AI-content policy page was found. The only AI item on the corporate site is a notice that an AI chat service ended (https://jtbpublishing.co.jp/news/IF000125), which is a service notice, not a policy. Intellectual-property terms: https://jtbpublishing.co.jp/intellectual_property/ (not read).
- **[inferred]** Listings follow one fixed data order, prices are shown tax-included (Japan has required 総額表示 since 2021), and a general note warns that hours and prices may change and should be confirmed before visiting.

**What this means for us.** Every service fact has the same precision across all items in a list. A correction is plain, specific and leaves a trace (our trace is the `updated` date and the editorial report). A data date belongs where a fact is time-sensitive, not as a decoration.

## 『暮しの手帖』

**Why it is the benchmark.** For app comparisons, this is the standard Japanese readers measure fairness against: the magazine that tests products and names brands, and refuses advertising so that it can.

**Editorial QC, in our words.**
- **[stated]** The magazine has never carried advertising and says it exists because readers buy each issue. Its product tests began as consumer goods spread in the growth years, with two aims: tell readers which products are actually useful, and push makers to improve. The tests take the consumer's viewpoint, rest on evidence, and name brands. The first test (socks) ran in 1954. (https://www.kurashi-no-techo.co.jp/company/)
- **[stated]** Corrections are published openly as お詫び or お詫びと訂正 notices on the site's news page, per issue (https://www.kurashi-no-techo.co.jp/blog/information/20260721 , https://www.kurashi-no-techo.co.jp/blog/information/20260910 ; notice bodies not read).
- **[third-party]** A reference dictionary explains the no-ads rule as protection against sponsor influence on content, which is why the tests are trusted (https://japanknowledge.com/introduction/keyword.html?i=47 ; it gives 1956 as a start date for the tests, against the publisher's own 1954, so treat the publisher as correct). The founder's refusal to bend to maker pressure is a theme of his biography (https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/318532).
- **[stated]** No AI policy or written editorial code was found on the site.
- **[inferred]** Tests apply the same conditions to every product, report weak points as plainly as strong ones, and explain the reason behind each judgement rather than giving a score alone.

**What this means for us.** We cannot be ad-free about VoyageLog; we make it. The honest equivalent is disclosure plus equal criteria, and giving VoyageLog's weak points the same plain treatment as everyone else's.

## Editor's lens: questions for every article

Ask these after the line edit. A "no" is a fix, not a note.

1. **一次情報 (primary source):** Was every competitor claim (無料範囲, アカウント要否, オフライン, 日本語対応, 価格) checked today on the app's own site and the JP storefront, and is each one logged in the report? The verification stays internal; no link or 「〜によると」 in the article.
2. **時点 (data date):** Would each changeable fact still be true six months after `updated`? If not, generalise it. Where the reader will act on a time-sensitive fact (Googleマップのタイムライン, a store price), anchor it once with 「2026年10月時点」 rather than 「現在」 or 「今日」, and make sure `updated` matches.
3. **取材の主張 (no false reporting):** Does any sentence imply we travelled, visited, ate, or tested an app on a trip? We checked pages and stores; say only that.
4. **同じ物差し (equal criteria):** In a comparison, are all apps judged on the same criteria, named before the first app, and are those criteria the ones this reader cares about?
5. **長所と短所 (both sides):** Does every app, VoyageLog included, get at least one concrete weak point? Is VoyageLog's limit (no route tracking, no sync, phones only) as specific as the competitors'?
6. **利害の開示 (disclosure):** Is 「VoyageLogは私たちが作っているアプリです」 stated once, plainly, before the first recommendation? Is VoyageLog's position in the list justified by a stated reason, not by ownership?
7. **選定理由 (why these, why this many):** Does the article say how the items were chosen (使われている, JP storeで入手できる, 条件に合う)? Is the count what the material supports rather than a 〇選 target?
8. **向かない人 (who should not choose it):** Does each recommendation say who it does not suit, and does the article keep the section that sends route-tracking readers elsewhere?
9. **確認していないこと (stated limits):** Where a gap exists (Android behaviour not checked, paid-tier feature not visible on the store page), is it either cut or stated plainly once, not hidden under 「〜かもしれません」?
10. **実用情報の精度 (service data):** For places and events, does the article keep only the stable facts (where, what, which season) and drop hours, prices and dates that rot? Is the same level of detail given for every item in a list?
11. **季節もの (seasonal):** No fixed 開花 or 見頃 dates; the reader is pointed to each place's or tourism body's 開花情報, named without a link.
12. **生活者の視点 (reader can verify):** Does the advice give the reader a way to check it themselves (機内モードで試す, 書き出してから機種変更する) instead of asking them to trust us?
13. **校閲 (proper nouns and figures):** App names exactly as the JP storefront spells them, feature names as `src/locales/ja.js` writes them, place names and kanji checked, prices 税込 in 円, numbers consistent between table, prose and FAQ.
14. **言い過ぎ (no overclaim):** Privacy and offline claims stay inside en.js and section 11 of the reference: 端末内に保存, no server, trip map offline with saved places only. Nothing about encryption, Face ID, sync or full offline maps.
15. **訂正の記録 (correction trail):** If this edit corrects something the published version got wrong, is the fix plain (the right fact, not a hedge), `updated` set, and the before/after recorded under "Removed or generalized" in the report?
16. **読者の質問 (real questions):** Are the FAQ questions ones Japanese readers actually type or ask, not product-marketing questions? Would each answer satisfy someone reading only that answer in search results?
17. **公正さ (the competitor test):** If the competitor's own team read their app's paragraph, could they point to a factual error or an unfair framing? If yes, fix it.
18. **広告の匂い (no affiliate tropes):** No ★ ratings, no 1位/2位 ranking language without criteria, no 【】, no 最新 claims, no urgency. The piece should read like a magazine test, not a sales page.
19. **独自性 (originality):** Is anything in structure, selection, headline or phrasing recognisable from a benchmark or a ranking site? If so, rewrite it in our own terms.

## What we never borrow

- Their text, headlines, captions, list selections, rankings, ratings or test results, in any form or paraphrase close enough to recognise.
- Their signature formats: guidebook listing blocks and icon systems, reader-tip boxes with contributor credits, test-report layouts and verdict labels, edition and data-date stamps in their style, series names or brand phrases.
- Their reporting claims: we do not 取材 on the ground, run lab tests, or collect reader submissions, so we never write as if we did.
- Their names: no mention, quote or link to any of these publications inside an article, the FAQ, the title or the description.
- Their authority: no 「専門誌も認める」 style borrowing of credibility. Our credibility comes from checked facts and stated limits.
