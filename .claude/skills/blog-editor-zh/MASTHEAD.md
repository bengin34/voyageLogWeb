# Masthead lens: Simplified Chinese desk

The bar for the zh desk, taken from how the most respected Chinese-language travel titles and one product-review site check their own work. Use it as a separate pass after the line edit (reference, section 10). It is a quality bar, not a source: nothing here goes into an article.

Researched 2026-10-24. Every policy page was read directly on the publisher's own site. The web-search budget ran out early in the session, so a few background facts (marked **background, not re-checked**) come from prior knowledge and need a quick check by the owner. **Stated** means the publisher says it on a public page. **Inferred** means we read it from their published work; they publish no policy on the point.

This lens is about editorial craft only: dating, sourcing, disclosure, test honesty, list logic. It takes no position on the publishers' owners, institutions or editorial lines.

## The benchmarks

### 1. 《中国国家地理》 (Chinese National Geography)

**Why it is the benchmark:** this is the reference title for places, landscapes and nature in mainland Chinese. Readers trust its place names, natural facts and seasons more than any travel brand's. It is active: its site carries September 2026 stories and a copyright line running to 2026.

**How it checks its work:**
- Stated: an academic team linked to its institutional sponsor (the Chinese Academy of Sciences) checks the scientific accuracy (科学性) of articles. The magazine also has written editorial and design norms for topic choice and writing style. It names exclusive, original, authoritative content as its basic promise, and it presents knowledge to readers as equals, not as a lecture.
- Stated (in its own history pieces): reader letters and feedback columns have long fed topic planning and editing. Editors ask whether readers want a piece and would pay for it.
- Inferred from published work: the reporting is first-hand, often long field trips. Captions credit each photographer (摄影/…) and texts credit the writer (撰文/…). Pieces carry a publication time. Specialist staff tighten captions that would otherwise be vague (one history piece jokes about the old, imprecise caption style).
- Not found: no public corrections policy, no AI policy, no sponsorship-disclosure rule. The site links to a magazine ad rate card, so paid advertising exists. How it is labelled is not stated.

**What to take:** precision about the physical world. Standard place names, checked seasons and natural facts, no decorative superlative that an expert would wince at.

- https://www.dili360.com/about/history/detail/id/11.htm (关于中国国家地理: accuracy team, editorial norms, mission)
- https://www.dili360.com/about/history/detail/id/10.htm and https://www.dili360.com/about/history/detail/id/5.htm (behind-the-scenes history: field reporting, reader feedback)

### 2. 孤独星球 Lonely Planet (Chinese-language guidebooks)

**Why it is the benchmark:** for practical outbound and domestic travel information, the Chinese editions of Lonely Planet are the guidebook many Chinese independent travellers grew up with. Its practical sections set the norm for what a service piece must contain. **Background, not re-checked:** the Simplified Chinese editions are published under licence by 中国地图出版社. No Chinese-language Lonely Planet website answered during research (lonelyplanet.com.cn and similar did not respond).

**How it checks its work (global brand pages; whether they bind the licensed Chinese editions is not stated):**
- Stated: recommendations come from staff, editors and contributors using their own judgement and reporting, and a named editor signs off every original piece.
- Stated: AI may help with data analysis, brainstorming, headline suggestions and tagging, always reviewed by an editor. AI is not used to write reporting, recommendations or reviews. It is not used to invent quotes, fake traveller experiences or simulate local expertise the brand does not have. Reader-facing AI summaries are labelled.
- Stated: writers may not take free or discounted services in exchange for coverage. Guidebook coverage based on press trips or freebies is not allowed. Pitches based on a press trip must say so up front.
- Stated: advertising that imitates editorial style without being clearly identifiable is refused. The publisher keeps sole editorial control.
- Stated: the brand accepts that facts on the ground change fast (restaurants close, prices move) and invites readers to report errors.
- Inferred from published work: articles show a date and a named writer. Practical information (getting around, best time to go, costs) sits in its own blocks, separate from inspiration, so it can be updated.

**What to take:** separate practical facts from atmosphere, date what changes, and never simulate first-hand experience or local knowledge.

Pages used:
- https://www.lonelyplanet.com/legal/ai-policy (Editorial Policy on AI: human sign-off, no fabricated experience, corrections invitation)
- https://www.lonelyplanet.com/about (contributor network, write-for-us guidelines, freebie and press-trip rules)
- https://www.lonelyplanet.com/legal/advertising-terms (editorial control, ads must be identifiable)
- https://www.lonelyplanet.com/articles/what-to-eat-and-drink-in-thailand (example of dating and bylines)

### 3. 《悦游 Condé Nast Traveler》

**Why it is the benchmark:** this is the reference for high-end Chinese travel and lifestyle writing, and for hotel, food and destination features with a strong point of view. It is already named in SKILL.md as the practical end of the standard. **Status caution:** the newest dated items on cntraveler.com.cn are from late 2022 and early 2023. Whether the print or social editions are still active was not verified this session. Treat it as a benchmark for craft, not as a sign of the current market.

**How it checks its work:**
- Stated (China about page): the magazine is published by 中国妇女杂志社 in a licensing partnership with Condé Nast, under the brand motto of truth in travel. Its annual readers' choice, Gold List and Hot List are its signature lists.
- Stated (global brand about page; whether it binds the China edition is inferred): writers are chosen for years of first-hand knowledge. Drafts go through several layers of editing, with several sources consulted where possible. Hotels and restaurants are chosen by editors and nearly all are reviewed by someone who stayed or ate there. Complimentary stays create no obligation to cover. A visible badge separates products the team tested from those it did not. For untested items, the page says what the selection relied on instead. Affiliate income is disclosed.
- Inferred from published work (China site): features carry a strong first-person voice and a named city or property focus. Service details (what to book, when) are woven into features rather than boxed.

**What to take:** say plainly what was tested and what was not, and what each choice rests on. A recommendation is an editor's judgement with stated criteria, not a list of everything available.

Pages used:
- https://www.cntraveler.com.cn/ (activity check: newest dates 2022/12 and 2023/01)
- https://www.cntraveler.com.cn/about.html (杂志介绍: publisher, motto, signature lists)
- https://www.cntraveler.com/info/about-us (global: commissioning, reviews process, tested vs untested, affiliate disclosure, badges)

### 4. 少数派 sspai (product and app reviews)

**Why it is the benchmark:** the most respected Chinese-language site for app and device reviews and app recommendations (App+1, 派评 roundups). Its readers overlap with ours: iPhone users who compare apps closely. It is active, with posts dated October 2026.

**How it checks its work:**
- Stated (user agreement): third-party and community content does not represent the site's position, and readers are told to judge it themselves. The site may send commercial messages through banners and notifications. No public review-standard, corrections or AI policy was found: the About page renders by script and its text could not be read, and the common policy paths returned 404.
- Inferred from published work:
  - Content types are labelled on the article card: 会员内容 (paid), Matrix精选 (community writers), 商店 (the site's own commerce posts), 派早报 (news). The reader always knows what kind of piece they are reading.
  - Reviews name the firmware or app version tested and the test conditions (device, codec, volume). They check the vendor's own claim against their own measurement and say where it falls short. They say how the device was obtained (bought on release).
  - Reviews end with a scoped verdict: who should get it, who already has enough with the built-in tool, and what remains a weakness in the current version.
  - Prices are given as "for now", with the official site named as the final word because prices change.
  - When a developer writes about their own app, the title says so (我做了一个……). The lede tells the story, and the text states plainly which platforms are not supported yet and what is free versus paid.

**What to take:** this is the closest model for our app articles. Disclose authorship in the first lines, scope every claim to a version and a storefront, set vendor claims apart from our judgement, and give a verdict that names who should not choose the app.

Pages used:
- https://sspai.com/page/agreement (user agreement)
- https://sspai.com/page/about-us (About page; text not readable without script)
- https://sspai.com/post/116398 (device review: version, test conditions, own measurement against vendor claim, scoped verdict)
- https://sspai.com/post/116445 (app review: built-in alternative is enough for some, dated price with official site as final word)
- https://sspai.com/post/115400 (developer's own app: authorship in title, platform limits, free vs Pro scope)
- https://sspai.com/api/v1/article/index/page/get?limit=20&offset=0 (public article list, used to read the content-type labels)

## Editor's lens: questions for every zh article

Ask these after the line edit, in order. A "no" is something to fix, or to record in the report if it cannot be fixed.

1. **一手来源 (primary source).** Can every fact about a competitor, price, season, place or rule be traced to a primary page recorded in the report? If it only came from a blog or a 小红书 post, it is not verified.
2. **时效 (shelf life).** Which facts will go stale: free tier, storefront availability, festival or blossom dates, booking rules? Each one is either verified today or written so it stays true ("每年花期不同，出发前看官方公告"). The `updated` date carries the article's date. Do not litter the body with 截至 hedges, and never use 截至 to cover a claim you did not check.
3. **实测边界 (test boundary).** Does any sentence imply we installed, tested, travelled or 亲测? We did not. Rewrite it to what we actually did ("我们看了各家的商店页和帮助页"), or cut it.
4. **范围 (scope).** Is each app claim scoped to what its own store page shows: platform, language, storefront (中国区 App Store, or "境外")? A claim without scope reads as universal and may be false in the mainland.
5. **厂商说法与我们的判断 (vendor claim vs our view).** Are vendor promises we cannot test framed as the vendor's own claim ("它自己说……"), and kept apart from our judgement? No sourcing language, no links.
6. **利益披露 (disclosure).** Is the one-line disclosure ("VoyageLog 是我们自己做的应用") in the first lines, as plain as a developer writing about their own app? Does the text also say, in the same plain register, what VoyageLog lacks (no auto-tracking, no cloud sync, no web)?
7. **入选理由 (why it is on the list).** Are the criteria stated before the list, and does each app get its reason? Is the obvious candidate a reader expects either included or excluded with a reason?
8. **同一把尺子 (same yardstick).** Are the same criteria applied to every app, ours included, with no straw man and no table cell left unverified?
9. **不适合谁 (who it is wrong for).** Does each recommendation, VoyageLog first, say who should not choose it? Does the route-tracking reader get sent to a tracker?
10. **不装精确 (no fake precision).** Are there scores, ranks, 第一, 最好用 or star grades? Reviewers who test can grade; we cannot. Replace them with stated fit ("适合只想记地点和评分的人").
11. **不编经历 (no invented experience).** Is every traveller scene framed as an example the reader could write ("比如在成都记一条：……"), not as something that happened to us? Are there no invented quotes, users or 网友说 lines?
12. **不冒充本地 (no simulated local knowledge).** Does any line claim insider or 私藏 knowledge we cannot stand behind (本地人才知道、隐藏打卡点)? Cut it or make it a checkable fact.
13. **地名与自然事实 (places and nature).** Are place names in their standard form? Are seasons, distances and natural facts checked against the official body? Is there a superlative (最美、第一、绝美) that an editor at a geography title would strike?
14. **服务性 (can the reader act).** After each section, could the reader do something specific, with feature names as they appear in the app (地点、瞬间、城市清单、导出)? Inspiration without a next step gets a step or gets cut.
15. **实用信息分开放 (practical facts kept apart).** Do practical facts (what the free version covers, what exports exist, what works offline) sit in short, clearly marked paragraphs or a verified table, not buried in mood text where they cannot be updated?
16. **市场现实 (market reality).** Is every app, store and service named usable from mainland China, or clearly marked as for readers abroad (Google 地图、Google Play)? No implied 高德 or 百度 support, no Chinese Android store recommendation.
17. **内容与转化分开 (content apart from the pitch).** Would the comparison still read as fair with the `{{cta}}` removed? Is VoyageLog praised in no more than the sections where it truly fits?
18. **标题兑现 (headline delivers).** Does the title promise exactly what the article gives, with no 超全, 必看, 天花板 or promise of more apps or detail than the body contains?
19. **易于更正 (easy to correct).** Is each changeable fact stated once, not repeated in body, table and FAQ in slightly different forms? Does the report list what to re-check and when (next year's blossom dates, a competitor's free tier)?

## What we never borrow

- **Their content:** no sentence, paragraph, caption, itinerary, tip or translated passage from any benchmark, in Chinese or any other language.
- **Their headlines** and headline formulas, including column-style prefixes like the developer-post or review-series labels.
- **Their rankings and lists:** Gold List, Hot List, readers' choice, Best in Travel, 中国最美的地方排行榜, 选美中国, or any list derived from them. Our selections come from our own criteria and our own checks.
- **Their list selections:** we do not pick the same apps, hotels, spots or cities because a benchmark did. Every app or place we include has its own reason in our text.
- **Their signature formats:** grade levels, stamps of approval and badges, column names (派评, App+1, 派早报, 一图流), 专辑 structures, "top experiences" blocks, Soundwalk-style series. No look-alike badges or seals of our own either.
- **Their recognisable phrasing:** mottos and slogans (truth in travel / 旅行之真谛, 推开自然之门……), and the house turns of phrase readers would recognise.
- **Their names inside articles:** never mention, quote, praise, compare with or link any benchmark publication in a VoyageLog article, FAQ, title or description. The lens lives in this file and in the editorial report only.
- **Their practices we cannot honestly follow:** no on-the-ground testing claims, no named single author (the byline stays plural 我们), no visible sourcing or links (house rule: verification stays internal and goes into `docs/editorial/zh.md`).
