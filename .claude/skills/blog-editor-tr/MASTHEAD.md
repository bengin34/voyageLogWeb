# Masthead lens: Turkish desk

The quality bar for the Turkish desk, taken from how the most respected Turkish travel and tech publications check their own work. It is a set of standards and questions. It is **not** a source of content (reference, section 10). Read it once per session, then run the "Editor's lens" questions on every article after the line edit.

Researched 2026-10-24. Every page used is listed with its URL so the owner can re-check it. Each statement is marked:
- **[stated]**: the publication's own published policy says this (paraphrased, never copied).
- **[inferred]**: no formal policy was found. The standard is inferred from the publication's published work as we know it, and was not re-checked page by page this session.

## The benchmarks

### 1. Atlas (gezi dergisi)

**Why it is the benchmark.** Atlas has been Turkey's reference travel and geography magazine since 1993. For decades it set the standard for travel reportage in Turkish: Anatolia and the wider world treated as serious subjects, careful place names, real photography and practical information a reader can act on. When a Turkish reader thinks of a trustworthy travel magazine, this is usually the one. It began in 1993 as a Doğan Burda Rizzoli monthly (early issues are dated May 1993 onward) and is now a digital title on the Bmag platform of Big Dijital Teknoloji A.Ş. The same catalogue lists Burda, Capital, Level and Ev Bahçe, which we know as former Doğan Burda titles **[inferred]**.

**Stated policy.** The platform's "Yayın İlkeleri" page is short and general **[stated]**. It promises the best quality it can manage, content that follows professional ethics, and respect for readers even when the work breaks conventions. It does not set out detailed rules on accuracy, corrections, sources, advertising or AI, and it does not mention the Basın Konseyi codes. We found no Atlas-specific ethics, corrections, fact-checking or AI policy.

**Lineage.** The fact that Atlas belonged to the Doğan group makes it likely that it worked under the Doğan Yayın Grubu "Yazılı Basın Yayın İlkeleri" (summarised under CHIP below) **[inferred]**. Treat those as the closest written version of its standard.

**What its service pieces consistently do [inferred].**
- They keep the narrative and the practical part apart. The story carries the experience. A separate, plain block carries how to get there, where to stay, when to go and what to watch for, so the reader can find the facts without rereading the prose.
- Places are named precisely and spelled correctly, in Turkish usage where one exists (Saraybosna, Batum, Tiflis). Districts, villages and valleys are named, not "a lovely village".
- Seasons are given as months and conditions ("ekimin ikinci yarısı, yağmurdan önce"), not "the best time of year".
- Local people are written as people, not scenery. Nobody is exoticised or reduced to their ethnicity or religion.
- The writer was there and the piece shows it. **We cannot borrow this** (see the lens, question 5).

### 2. CHIP Türkiye (product and tech reviews)

**Why it is the benchmark.** CHIP is one of the longest-running Turkish technology magazines. It is known for product tests and comparisons rather than rewritten press releases, and it is the only Turkish tech outlet we found that links a full written publishing code. Our app-comparison articles (en iyi seyahat günlüğü uygulamaları, Polarsteps alternatifi) compete in its territory: a reader choosing software and expecting honest criteria. CHIP is now published by Big Medya Teknoloji A.Ş. The Doğan code it still links reflects its Doğan Burda past **[inferred]**.

**Stated policy [stated].** CHIP links the Doğan Yayın Grubu "Yazılı Basın Yayın İlkeleri" (a PDF of shared values plus numbered rules for newspapers and magazines) from its site. In our own words, the parts that matter for us:
- **Accuracy before speed.** Speed, exaggeration or simplification must never push out the full truth. The publication should say openly what it does not know and avoid speculation.
- **Verification.** A story is published only after it has been checked with every means available, and the piece either says what that check found or the facts are certain. Information found online needs checking at its origin like any other.
- **Corrections.** Errors are admitted openly and fixed as fast as possible. There are mechanisms to stop the same error coming back, and the right of reply and correction is respected.
- **Commercial separation.** Advertising, sponsorship and paid content are marked so that no reader can mistake them. Company and product names appear only when they belong to the story. Nothing is published on an advertiser's suggestion.
- **Conflicts of interest.** Staff keep their work free of any interest or influence. Outside contributors tell readers about any financial interest in, or affiliation with, the people or companies they write about.
- **Trips and gifts.** The publication pays for its own reporting trips. Invited trips need an editor's approval, and if one becomes a story, the piece says it was an invitation. Gifts and favours that break professional ethics are refused.
- **Headlines.** A headline must not say something the content does not support.
- **Quotes and surveys.** Quotes are not cut so that they mislead, and they carry their source and date. A survey reported in a story names who ran it, who paid, when, the sample size and the method.
- **Images.** Archive photos, animations and reconstructions are always labelled as such.
- **Fairness.** Editors should put themselves in the other side's place and judge whether they were fair. Nobody is mocked or defined by ethnicity, religion, language, class or disability unless it is the news.
- **Privacy.** Photos of private people are not taken or published without permission unless there is real news value.

We found no CHIP page called "nasıl test ediyoruz" or anything like it, no test-lab entry in the masthead, and no AI-content policy.

**What its comparisons consistently do [inferred].**
- They name the criteria before the verdict, and the verdict depends on the use case ("best for X"), not one absolute winner.
- Each product gets its weak points in plain words next to its strengths.
- Prices and versions are tied to a point in time, because both change.
- Products are compared within the same class. A free tool is not judged against a professional suite without saying so.

- https://www.chip.com.tr/kunye (publisher Big Medya Teknoloji A.Ş.; no test-lab role listed)

**Why it matters.** It is the Turkish edition of the most cited magazine for maps, captions and checked figures, and as far as we know it was also published in Turkey by Doğan Burda **[inferred]**. Its standard is precision: every number, place name and caption is checked.

**Status.** nationalgeographic.com.tr now redirects to the National Geographic TV channel site (natgeotv.com/tr). We could not find the magazine's own Turkish site or any Turkish editorial policy. ## Editor's lens: questions for every Turkish article

Run this pass after the line edit. Each question is adapted to what we are: an app maker writing for search, with a plural byline ("biz"), no on-the-ground testing, and no external links or citations. Where a benchmark would show its source, we verify internally and log the check in `docs/editorial/tr.md`.

1. **Başlık sözünü tutuyor mu?** Does the title (and H1, description) promise only what the body delivers? A "en iyi" title needs criteria in the body. A "nasıl" title needs steps a reader can follow.
2. **Is the disclosure plain and early?** One sentence in the first or second paragraph saying we make VoyageLog. Contributors who have an interest say so; we are the interested party in every comparison.
3. **Is every competitor claim checked today?** Check the app's own page and its Turkish storefront (`apps.apple.com/tr/...`, Google Play `hl=tr&gl=TR`), with the URL in the report. If not checked, make the claim generic or cut it.
4. **Do we admit what we do not know?** No "muhtemelen", "sanırım", "genellikle böyledir" guesses. Either a verified fact, or a plain "bunu gitmeden kontrol edin", or nothing.
5. **Does any sentence imply we were there or tested on a trip?** Atlas writers travel; we do not claim to. "Denedik", "kullandık", "gezimizde fark ettik" become what we actually did ("uygulamaların kendi sayfalarına baktık") or go. Example entries are clearly examples.
6. **Is every list justified?** The criteria come before the list, and each app or place is on it for a reason the reader can check. If a selection rests on nothing but our taste, say so, or cut the list down to what we can defend.
7. **Would the competitor's maker call our description fair?** Put yourself in their place. Name what they do better (route tracking, sharing, printed books) as plainly as what we do better.
8. **Are VoyageLog's limits stated where they matter?** No automatic route, no cloud sync, no web or iPad claim, no planner (city lists aside). The "VoyageLog ne zaman yanlış seçim" section and any sentence that sends route-trackers elsewhere stay.
9. **Is time-sensitive practical information safe?** Visa rules, kur and prices, opening months, ferry and train times, market dates: verified today on the official source, or written as a check-before-you-go instruction. Seasons are months and conditions, not "en güzel zamanı". If facts changed, `updated` changes.
10. **Are numbers exact and accounted for?** Every statistic has a who and a when (in the report, not the article), with Turkish number format. No rounding up for effect. Competitor prices only in TL from today's Turkish store page; usually "ücretli bir seçeneği de var" is the better sentence.
11. **Is each brand name earning its place?** Name a company or product only when the reader needs it to act. Drop brand names that are only there as decoration or keywords.
12. **Is the commercial part unmistakable?** One `{{cta}}`, and advice sections without sales copy. Pro-only features are marked as Pro. The free tier is described honestly (no trip number, no price).
13. **Is the service part findable?** Practical steps, settings and checklists can be scanned without reading the narrative: short sections, concrete headings, steps in order. Use our own structure, not a benchmark's box format.
14. **Would the reader succeed by following it literally?** Every step matches the app as `src/locales/en.js` describes it and uses the Turkish labels from `src/locales/tr.js`.
15. **Are places named precisely and correctly?** Districts, valleys and stations rather than "a nice spot". Turkish exonyms where they exist (Saraybosna, Batum, Tiflis, Prag), TDK spelling, verified place facts.
16. **Are people treated with respect?** Locals are not scenery and are not defined by ethnicity or religion. Example notes do not expose identifiable private people, and advice about photos of strangers respects their privacy.
17. **Is the article original?** Could a heading, a list selection, a ranking or a turn of phrase be recognised as Atlas, Skylife, CHIP, a Hürriyet piece or a competitor's blog? If so, rewrite it in our own words and structure.
18. **Is a correction traceable?** If the edit fixes a fact that was published wrong, the report says what was wrong and what it became, so the owner can decide whether the `updated` date is enough.

## What we never borrow

- Their content: text, facts gathered by their reporters, photos, maps, captions, interviews.
- Their headlines, standfirsts and section titles, including near-paraphrases.
- Their rankings, scores, test seals, "best for" picks and list selections. Our lists come from our own criteria and our own checks.
- Their signature formats: Atlas's practical-information box as a fixed template, CHIP's scoring tables and plus/minus boxes, any recurring column name or rubric.
- Recognisable phrasing, slogans and stock expressions associated with them.
- Any mention, quote, link or "… göre" reference to them inside an article, including the FAQ and the frontmatter.
- Practices we cannot honestly follow: claims of on-the-ground testing, named single authors, lab scores.
