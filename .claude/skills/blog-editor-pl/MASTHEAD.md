# Masthead lens: Polish desk (pl)

A quality bar taken from how the strongest travel and product-review titles in the Polish market check their work. It is **not a source of content**: nothing below may appear in an article, and these publications are never named, quoted or linked in one (reference, section 10). Use it as a separate pass after the line edit (see `SKILL.md`, "Masthead lens").

Researched 2026-10-24. Every URL below is a page actually read that day, so the owner can re-check it. **Stated** means the publication says it on a policy or about page. **Inferred** means we read it off published articles; it is a habit, not a written rule, and may change.

How the research went: web search was not available in this session, so pages were reached by direct fetch. Komputer Świat, Onet Podróże, Wyborcza.pl and Burda's policy PDFs (mojedane.burdamedia.pl) could not be fetched. The Sejm legal database (ISAP) showed a human-verification page, so the press-law points below were not re-read this session. Polish publishers rarely publish newsroom codes: none of the sites checked has a public editorial-standards, fact-checking, corrections or AI-use policy for its own journalism. Most of what follows is therefore inferred from their published work, and is marked as such.

## Baseline every Polish newsroom works under

Not a publication but the shared floor that Polish readers and editors expect, so it sets the minimum for the lens.

- **Prawo prasowe** (press law, 1984, as amended). From the statute as generally known; **not re-read this session**: re-check on ISAP. Journalists must take particular care and diligence (szczególna staranność i rzetelność) when gathering material, in particular checking that information is true or naming its source. They must look after correct language. Advertising must be marked so that nobody mistakes it for editorial material. A published factual claim can be met with a formal correction (sprostowanie). URL tried: https://isap.sejm.gov.pl/isap.nsf/DocDetails.xsp?id=WDU19840050024
- **Karta Etyczna Mediów** (Media Ethics Charter, signed 29 March 1995 by the main journalists' associations, public broadcasters, Polsat and the press publishers' union). Seven principles: truth, objectivity, separating information from comment, honesty, respect and tolerance, the reader's interest first, freedom with responsibility. Read on a **secondary** source (Wikipedia; it lists only the principle names, and the council's own site no longer carries the text): https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karta_Etyczna_Mediów

## Benchmark 1: National Geographic Traveler (Polish edition)

**Why it is the benchmark:** the best-known travel title in Poland, the local edition of an international brand, published by Burda Media Polska alongside National Geographic Polska. Its print masthead is the reference point for what a "serious" travel magazine looks like to Polish readers. Its website mixes that standard with traffic-driven news, which makes it useful both as a bar and as a warning.

**Stated:**
- A full masthead with separate roles that shows how many hands touch a page: editor-in-chief, lead editor and editorial secretary, editors, photo editors, a cartographer, a dedicated proofreader (korektor). No fact-checker role is listed. https://www.national-geographic.pl/kontakt/
- The terms and the footer reserve the publisher's rights against using the content to train AI or for text-and-data mining without explicit consent. This is a rights notice, not a policy on how the newsroom itself uses AI. https://www.national-geographic.pl/regulamin/ (the linked PDF at mojedane.burdamedia.pl could not be fetched)
- Not found: editorial standards, corrections policy, independence statement, AI-use policy for its own content. Home page footer checked: https://www.national-geographic.pl/

**Inferred from published work** (Traveler section https://www.national-geographic.pl/traveler/ and two service pieces, a Poznań weekend guide and a Christmas-markets list):
- Named author on every piece with a short bio that establishes why this person can write it. A publication timestamp, but no "updated" date.
- Time-sensitive facts carry the year. A closure is stated with its start date and the expected reopening, not as "currently closed".
- Genuine uncertainty is named as such: a disputed historical point is called disputed, an estimate is called an estimate.
- Commercial content is marked: a general affiliate-links note, and every paid sidebar item carries „Współpraca reklamowa”.
- A ranking taken from a third-party vote is credited to that vote, not presented as the magazine's own judgement.
- Where it falls short (do not copy the weakness): curiosity-gap web headlines that withhold the answer, list selections whose criteria are only implied, superlatives and visitor figures with no stated basis, event windows given only as a rough season.

## Benchmark 2: Podróże (podroze.pl, Grupa ZPR Media)

**Why it is the benchmark:** a large Polish travel portal under the "Podróże" name, aimed squarely at service journalism: where to go, when, how to get there, what to check first, on a budget. It is the closest match to the search intent our articles serve.

**Stated:**
- The about page states the mission (help readers plan trips more simply, cheaply and confidently) and says the team produces the site with AI tools, with material prepared and verified for readers' practical needs. It does not describe the verification process, and says nothing about independence or how advertising is labelled. https://podroze.pl/redakcja/o-serwisie-aa-NSQ6-MQwj-pvck.html
- Not found: editorial standards, corrections, ethics. Footer checked: https://podroze.pl/

**Inferred from published work** (a Giewont-with-children route guide and an autumn heritage-railways roundup):
- Practical facts carry the year or the season they apply to, and the operator is named.
- The reader is told where to confirm before travelling: check the current timetable with the operator, book ahead when seats are limited. This turns uncertainty into an action instead of a hedge.
- Safety comes before inspiration: crowding, wet rock, chains, storms, the right footwear, and a plain statement of who should not attempt the route.
- Advice branches by the reader's situation (the child's age decides the route), instead of one answer for everyone.
- AI-generated lead images are labelled as AI in the caption.
- Where it falls short: no "last checked" date, prices given without a year, selection criteria implied rather than stated, some "oldest" and "most popular" claims with no basis.
- Pages read: https://podroze.pl/polska/z-dziecmi/wejscie-na-giewont-z-dzieckiem-jak-zaplanowac-trase-na-szczyt-spiacego-rycerza-aa-5h1U-X1nc-dS8m.html and https://podroze.pl/teraz/sezon/sezon-jeszcze-sie-nie-skonczyl-tymi-zabytkowymi-kolejkami-mozna-przejechac-sie-jesienia-aa-9qri-e4cC-XkAf.html

## Benchmark 3 (product reviews): CHIP.pl

**Why it is the benchmark:** one of the oldest Polish technology brands (the site states almost 30 years of history), with a dedicated testing desk. It is the relevant bar for our app-comparison and alternatives articles. Komputer Świat would be the other candidate but could not be fetched. Federacja Konsumentów was checked for product tests and methodology; none appear in its site navigation, so it was not used.

**Stated:**
- A public masthead naming the editor-in-chief, a head of testing (Testy) and the editors. https://www.chip.pl/nasza-redakcja
- Not found: a "how we test" page, test methodology, ethics or independence policy, AI policy. Home page footer checked: https://www.chip.pl/

**Inferred from published work** (a long smartphone test and a brand-supported travel impressions piece, both 23 October 2026):
- The label tells the reader what kind of piece it is. A full test is called a test; a lighter piece is called impressions (wrażenia). A piece made with a brand carries „Lokowanie produktu” with the brand's name, at the top and again above the author box.
- The review says what it could not establish (battery life it could not confirm with confidence, a charger it did not have) and that software updates may change the findings.
- The verdict says who the product is for and who it is not for, including the reviewer's own "not for me", rather than a single score.
- Prices are given with a caveat that they move, but without the date they were checked or where.
- Where it falls short: it does not say whether the unit was lent by the manufacturer, and the test period is implied rather than stated.

Also checked, not used as benchmarks: Polityka (https://www.polityka.pl/) and Tygodnik Powszechny publish no editorial standards; Tygodnik Powszechny's about page states independence from political groups and corporations and reader funding (https://tygodnikpowszechny.pl/o-nas-25466). They remain the voice benchmark in `SKILL.md`, not a QC benchmark. Kontynenty could not be verified: kontynenty.pl does not resolve and kontynenty.net is an unrelated personal site.

## Editor's lens: questions for every VoyageLog article

Ask each one after the line edit. A "no" is a fix in the article or a line in the report.

1. **Checked today, on whose page?** For every competitor, price, season, event and place fact: can you name in the report the primary source you read today (Polish App Store or Google Play page, the operator, the venue)? If not, it goes (reference, section 5). Sourcing lives in the report, never in the article.
2. **Does the date tell the truth?** If any fact, section or more than a few sentences changed, `updated` is today. Seasonal and time-bound facts in the body carry their year or season („w sezonie 2026”, „od listopada do początku stycznia”), so the reader can judge freshness without a footnote.
3. **Does uncertainty become an action, not a hedge?** Where details really do change (timetables, store prices, opening days), tell the reader what to check before going („sprawdź aktualny rozkład u przewoźnika”) as plain advice, with no link and no „według”. Never leave „prawdopodobnie” or „chyba” on a fact.
4. **Are the selection criteria on the page?** Every list or comparison says in one sentence why these items and not others (for example: available in the Polish stores, works offline, no account). An implied selection is the benchmarks' weak spot; ours is explicit.
5. **Is our interest declared like product placement?** The disclosure that we make VoyageLog appears once, plainly, near the top of every comparison or alternatives piece, and the article never poses as a neutral ranking. If VoyageLog sits first, the article gives the reason, not just the position.
6. **Does it claim experience we did not have?** No „przetestowaliśmy”, „używaliśmy przez miesiąc”, „byliśmy tam”, no first-hand impressions of competitor apps or places. Facts are stated as facts about the app or place, from what we verified. The benchmarks' test diaries are exactly the practice we do not borrow.
7. **Are fact and opinion separated?** A feature is a fact; „lepszy do…” is our judgement and reads as ours, with its reason in the same sentence. No opinion dressed as a statistic.
8. **Who is it for, and who is it not for?** Each app in a comparison gets the reader it fits and the reader it does not. The „kiedy VoyageLog to zły wybór” section is real: route trackers go to a tracker, cloud-sync and web users are told plainly.
9. **Are product limits stated as plainly as features?** No automatic route tracking, no cloud sync, no web or iPad app claimed, data only on the phone. Each limit appears where the reader would hit it, not buried at the end.
10. **Is every risk paired with its fix?** Where the reader can lose something (photos, a phone with the only copy of a journal, roaming outside the EU, a mountain trail in October), the article names the risk once and the concrete step that prevents it (JSON backup, export, checking conditions).
11. **Does the advice branch by situation?** One trip a year or twenty, iPhone or Android, travelling with children or alone, printing a book or only keeping a PDF. If the answer depends on the reader, the article says on what.
12. **Is every superlative earned?** „Najpopularniejszy”, „najstarszy”, „najlepszy”, „jedyny” are verified on a primary source today or cut. The same goes for user counts and ratings, which our source never has.
13. **Does the headline keep its promise?** The title says what the reader gets and the first paragraph delivers it. No curiosity-gap teasers that withhold the answer, no „nie uwierzysz”, no „TOP” framing borrowed from news sites.
14. **Is a disputed fact handled honestly?** If something is genuinely contested or varies by region or storefront, the article says so in plain words or leaves it out. It never silently picks a side.
15. **Would a price survive until next week?** Prices only from the Polish storefront today, in złoty, and only if they add something. Promotions, launch discounts and "from" prices stay out; „darmowa z płatną wersją” is usually the better sentence.
16. **Is everything named actually available in Poland?** Every app, store, photo-book service or feature is available to a reader in Poland, with Polish interface claims checked on the store listing.
17. **Would a correction be visible to the owner?** If this edit fixes a fact the published version got wrong (especially a competitor claim), the report says what was wrong and what replaced it: our equivalent of a sprostowanie.
18. **Would a korektor pass it?** Commas before subordinate clauses, numeral agreement, „…” quotes, lowercase ty-forms, no półpauza as a sentence dash. Correct Polish is a professional duty, not polish.

## What we never borrow

- Their articles, paragraphs, sentences or recognisable phrasing, in Polish or translated.
- Their headlines or headline formulas (the withheld-answer teaser, the "not X, not Y, it is Z" opener).
- Their rankings, list selections, "top N" picks or the order of their lists, including rankings they took from third-party votes.
- Their signature formats and series names (named columns, recurring weekend-guide or test templates, verdict badges, scoring schemes).
- Their test results, measurements, impressions or prices as if they were ours.
- Their photos, maps, captions or graphics.
- Any mention of them in an article: no names, no quotes, no links, no „jak piszą…”.
- Practices we cannot honestly follow: named individual bylines (we stay „my”), on-the-ground or hands-on testing claims, visible source links (house rule: verification stays in the report).
