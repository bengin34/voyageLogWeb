# Masthead lens: Italian desk

The quality bar for the Italian desk, taken from how the most trusted Italian travel publishers and one product-testing benchmark check their own work. Read it with `REFERENCE.md` section 10: borrow the standard, never the content.

Research done on 2026-10-24 on each publisher's own pages. Every point is marked:
- **[stated]**: the publisher says it in a published policy, method or about page (URL given).
- **[inferred]**: we read it off their published work, not off a policy. Treat it as a pattern, not as their rule.

Re-check the URLs before relying on a detail; policies move.

## 1. The benchmarks

### Touring Club Italiano (Touring Editore, rivista Touring, Bandiere arancioni)

**Why it is the benchmark.** The oldest Italian travel institution (founded 1894), a non-profit foundation whose publishing arm calls itself the reference point for Italian travel publishing: the guide rosse, maps and atlases, the member magazine. For an Italian reader, "Touring says so" still means checked.

**How it checks its work.**
- [stated] It describes itself as independent but taking positions (« indipendente ma non neutrale »): it judges openly, in the interest of the country and the traveller, not of the places it covers. https://www.touringclub.it/chi-siamo/mission
- [stated] Its Bandiera arancione quality mark shows how it justifies a selection: a published model with more than 250 criteria in five named areas, anonymous visits by its own assessors, a written improvement plan for every town assessed whatever the outcome, and a mark that is temporary, re-verified every three years and can be withdrawn. Paid promotion for certified towns is a separate, optional step after the assessment. https://www.touringclub.it/cosa-facciamo/bandiere-arancioni/liniziativa
- [stated] The publishing arm frames its job as giving Italians tools for informed, quality travel. https://www.touringclub.it/cosa-facciamo/produzioni-editoriali
- [not found] No public editorial code, corrections policy or AI policy for the magazine or the guides on the pages we could reach (https://www.touringclub.it/chi-siamo). Do not attribute one to them.

**The lens we take.** Any list is justified by criteria a reader could check. Verdicts expire and are re-checked. Judgment is open, and separate from anything commercial.

### Dove (Corriere della Sera / RCS)

**Why it is the benchmark.** A leading Italian monthly travel magazine, published within the Corriere della Sera group and marking about 35 years (dove35anni.corriere.it), with a large web edition at viaggi.corriere.it. It sets the tone for Italian service travel writing: elegant, place-led, practical.

**How it checks its work.**
- [stated, Corriere della Sera] The Corriere code of ethics asks for accuracy reached by checking facts and sources, using more than one source where possible, clearly separating first-hand from second-hand sources, and avoiding exaggeration. Errors are corrected as fast as possible, with the correction noted at the foot of the online article. Editorial decisions are taken independently of shareholders, and ownership and funding (sales and advertising) are disclosed. https://www.corriere.it/trust/codice_etico.shtml
- [inferred] That the Corriere code binds Dove's web edition is our reading: Dove runs on the Corriere domain, but the code names the Corriere della Sera, and we found no Dove-specific policy page. viaggi.corriere.it blocks automated reading of most pages; only its community and cookie policies are linked from the home page.
- [inferred] Service pieces carry a named author and a publication date, open with a standfirst (catenaccio) saying what the reader will get (where to go, what to eat), add an index for long pieces, and flag what changes: seasonal timetables are given with a note to check them before leaving. Example read: https://viaggi.corriere.it/itinerari-e-luoghi/sulcis-iglesiente/
- [not found] No public AI policy for Dove or the Corriere code at the time of research.

**The lens we take.** First-hand versus second-hand is always clear to the reader. No exaggeration. Practical facts that move are either checked today or handed to the reader as something to check. A mistake gets fixed and recorded.

### Lonely Planet Italia (EDT)

**Why it is the benchmark.** EDT publishes the Italian edition of Lonely Planet (stated in the lonelyplanetitalia.it footer), a guidebook series widely used by Italian independent travellers, and runs lonelyplanetitalia.it. Its reputation rests on one idea: someone went.

**How it checks its work.**
- [stated, Lonely Planet global] Recommendations come from contributors' own judgment and first-hand reporting, and writers may not accept free or discounted services in exchange for coverage. https://www.lonelyplanet.com/about
- [stated, Lonely Planet global] Its AI policy: AI does not write original reporting, reviews or recommendations; it is never used to invent quotes, fake traveller experiences or simulate local expertise the publisher lacks; a named editor signs off on every original piece; AI-made summaries of its own content are labelled; readers are invited to report what has changed, because places close and prices move. https://www.lonelyplanet.com/legal/ai-policy
- [inferred] That these global policies govern the Italian edition is our reading: lonelyplanetitalia.it links no editorial policy of its own (only legal, privacy and credits pages, https://www.lonelyplanetitalia.it/icons-media-credits), but it publishes Lonely Planet authors' articles under their names.
- [inferred] Articles show a named author, the publication month, photo credits, and practical tips set apart from the narrative. Example read: https://www.lonelyplanetitalia.it/articoli/consigli-di-viaggio/cosa-fare-in-lapponia-in-autunno

**The lens we take.** Never fake experience or expertise, with or without AI. Every piece has someone who answers for it. Tell the reader that things change.

### Altroconsumo (product-review benchmark for app comparisons)

**Why it is the benchmark.** One of Italy's main consumer organisations, independent by its own account and funded mostly by members, and the reference for comparative tests (its « Migliore del test » and « Miglior acquisto » titles). Our app comparisons are product reviews, and Altroconsumo sets the Italian standard for how a fair comparison explains itself.

**How it checks its work.**
- [stated] A general test-method page explains how products are chosen (market leaders plus store brands, novelties, heavily advertised products and readers' suggestions), that products are bought anonymously and never accepted as samples from manufacturers, that a product about to change before publication is left out, that labs declare conflicts of interest, that weights are set per category and some failed tests cap the overall score, and exactly what each title means. https://www.altroconsumo.it/vita-privata-famiglia/servizi-e-contratti/speciali/metodo-test
- [stated] Category pages publish the weighting (for smartphones: photo 25%, battery 15%, screen 15%, durability 10%), say where prices were recorded, and that the models were tested within the last year. https://www.altroconsumo.it/hi-tech/smartphone/come-testiamo
- [stated] Method pages state the limits of a score (a label-based score is not an absolute judgment) and when data were collected. https://www.altroconsumo.it/alimentazione/colazione/biscotti-come-testiamo
- [stated] Funding is disclosed, including the commercial income streams. https://www.altroconsumo.it/chisiamo/come-ci-finanziamo
- [inferred] Its lighter app reviews are dated and name the platform used, but give no scoring protocol (example: https://www.altroconsumo.it/hi-tech/intelligenza-artificiale/recensioni/app-copilot-ai). The full method applies to lab tests, not to every article.
- [not found] No corrections policy or AI-content policy on the pages read.

**The lens we take.** Say how the comparison was made before giving the verdict. Same yardstick for every product. Say what the verdict does not cover. Disclose your interest.

## 2. Editor's lens: questions for every Italian article

Ask these after the line edit. Each turns a benchmark standard into something we can honestly do: we check primary pages, we do not test on the ground, we never link out, we sign as « noi ».

1. **Date of truth.** Was every fact that can change (an app's free tier, price, platform, a feature, a ticket rule, a season) checked on its primary source during this edit? If not, is it generalized or gone, and is `updated` set when facts moved?
2. **First-hand or second-hand.** Can the reader tell what we know as the makers of VoyageLog, what is a competitor's own claim (« Polarsteps dice che … »), and what is general knowledge? Attribution happens in the sentence, never as a citation or link.
3. **No borrowed experience.** Does any sentence suggest we travelled with the apps, tested them on trips, or spent weeks with them? Change it to what we did (we read each app's pages and its store listing in Italy) or cut it.
4. **No invented people.** No made-up reader quotes, traveller anecdotes presented as real, « una nostra lettrice ci ha scritto », or local expertise we do not have. An example note is fine when it reads as an example.
5. **Why these.** Can the reader see why these apps (cities, prompts) are in the list and others are not? Is the criterion stated in a sentence (a journal and not a planner, available in the Italian stores, usable in Italian), and does every item meet it?
6. **Criteria before verdict.** In a comparison, are the criteria named before the « migliore » and does the verdict follow from them? If privacy is the yardstick, the winner cannot be crowned on features.
7. **Same yardstick for VoyageLog.** Is VoyageLog judged by the same criteria as the others, including the ones it loses (no route tracking, no web, no sync)? Is « quando VoyageLog non è la scelta giusta » specific enough to send someone elsewhere?
8. **Interest disclosed.** Is « VoyageLog lo facciamo noi » in the first or second paragraph, plain, once? Does any passage that praises VoyageLog read as fair given that disclosure?
9. **Known absentees.** If an app Italian readers would expect is missing, is there a one-line reason (not a journal, not in the Italian stores, changing too fast to describe)? Or is the omission quietly creating a false picture?
10. **Limits said aloud.** Does the article say what its advice does not cover (one platform only, depends on the reader's habit, store details change)? One honest sentence, not a hedge on every line.
11. **Hand over the check.** For practical facts that move fast (opening hours, access fees, app prices), does the article either state them as checked today or tell the reader to confirm on the official site before leaving, without a link or source name?
12. **Precision or nothing.** Is every number exact and sourced (en.js or the primary page), or removed? No « circa » on a figure nobody measured, no rounding from memory, no implied counts of users or downloads.
13. **No exaggeration.** Is each superlative tied to a criterion the reader can see? « La migliore per chi non vuole un account » stands; « la migliore in assoluto » does not.
14. **The promise matches.** Do title, description and first paragraph promise only what the body delivers? A standfirst-level promise (what you will be able to do after reading) is the test.
15. **Each section earns a step.** Does each H2 leave the reader with something to do or decide, in the tu form? Atmosphere without a usable point gets cut or tightened.
16. **Italian reality.** Are the examples Italian and current (a regionale in Liguria, Ticino roaming outside the EU, Apple Maps or Google Maps links), and is every service named actually available and in use in Italy?
17. **Separation of judgment and sales.** Is the {{cta}} the only commercial moment? No feature pitch inside a comparison table, no competitor criticised to make VoyageLog look better than the facts allow.
18. **Accountable voice.** Is the article consistently « noi », with no first-person singular persona (« sono cresciuta a … ») and no invented named author? Our sign-off is the editorial note in `docs/editorial/it.md`.
19. **Correction recorded.** If this edit corrects a fact that a published version stated, is the before and after written in the report so the owner can see it? (Visible correction notes in the article are the owner's call, not the editor's.)

## 3. What we never borrow

- Their text, in any amount beyond a few common words; no paraphrase that follows one of their paragraphs sentence by sentence.
- Their headlines, standfirsts, title formulas or recognisable phrasing.
- Their rankings, list selections, itineraries, « migliori » picks or the order they put things in.
- Their signature formats and labels: Touring's Bandiera arancione or guide rosse framing, Dove's named columns, Lonely Planet's tip boxes and « Best in Travel » style lists, Altroconsumo's titles (« Migliore del test », « Miglior acquisto »), scores or star systems.
- Their test results or scores as facts about an app; we check the app's own pages instead.
- Their names: never mention, quote, praise, compare with or link to any of these publications inside an article, its title, description or FAQ.
- Practices we cannot honestly follow: on-the-ground testing, anonymous purchases, lab results, named reviewers. We borrow only the honesty behind them.
