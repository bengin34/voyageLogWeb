# Masthead lens: Arabic-language market

The quality bar for the Arabic desk, taken from how respected Arabic-language travel and general-interest publishers check their own work. It is a set of standards, never a source of content (reference, section 10). Researched on 2026-10-24. Everything below is paraphrased; quotes are a few words at most. Each claim is marked **stated** (the publisher says it on the page listed) or **inferred** (read from their published work, not from a written policy). The lens covers editorial craft only: it takes no view on any publisher's ownership, politics or religion. Re-check the URLs before relying on a detail; Arabic URLs are given decoded so they can be read.

What exists in this market, in short: none of the publishers read here publishes a full editorial code, a review test method or an AI-content policy. One publishes its corrections, two describe their editing chain. Most of the standard below is therefore inferred from their published service pieces, and says so.

## 1. ناشيونال جيوغرافيك العربية (Abu Dhabi Media; ngalarabiya.com)

**Why it is the benchmark.** The Arabic edition of National Geographic, published from Abu Dhabi since October 2010, and the reference for serious, carefully edited Arabic writing about places, nature and travel (its travel section is called «ترحال»). `SKILL.md` already names it as the tone standard. The old domain ngarabia.com now redirects to ngalarabiya.com.

**Editorial QC, in our words**
- **Stated:** most of what it publishes is translated from the parent magazine, and pieces about the Arab region are written and photographed by Arab contributors; the magazine also says it works with an advisory board of Arab scientists. It says plainly what is translated and what is original. (https://ngalarabiya.com/about-us)
- **Stated:** it keeps a public corrections page, «تنويه وتصويب», listing errors issue by issue as a pair: the wrong text and the right text, with the page number, and invites readers to send corrections by email. The errors listed are small and telling: a misspelt word on a cover, a photo caption with left and right swapped, a photographer's gender wrong in the credit. (https://ngalarabiya.com/clarifications)
- **Stated:** the masthead lists a dedicated review and proofreading role («المراجعة والتدقيق») separate from editing and layout. (https://ngalarabiya.com/staff)
- **Inferred (from travel pieces):** every article carries a byline and a publication date; every photo carries a credit; a service piece is built on the questions a visitor actually asks (how to get around, what else to see, when to go, where to stay), each answered with concrete facts (counts, distances, months) and with advice attributed to a named person and their role. (https://ngalarabiya.com/category/travel, https://ngalarabiya.com/article/4340359/أسباب-جديدة-لزيارة-مصر-الآن, https://ngalarabiya.com/article/4345275/كيف-تتجنب-تأثيرات-فارق-التوقيت-خلال-السفر)
- **Inferred (a lesson, not a criticism):** a December 2022 travel piece describing a museum opening as imminent is still served unchanged. A publication date protects the reader only if time-bound words ("imminent", "new", "this year") are updated or avoided.
- **Not found:** no editorial code, sponsorship rule or AI policy on the pages read.

## 2. الشرق الأوسط, travel desk «سفر وسياحة» (SRMG; aawsat.com)

**Why it is the benchmark.** The pan-Arab daily, printed in many cities and read across the Gulf, the Levant, North Africa and the diaspora, with a weekly-rhythm travel section inside «يوميات الشرق». It is the closest benchmark for our reader: one Arabic text that has to work from Riyadh to Casablanca.

**Editorial QC, in our words**
- **Stated:** the paper describes its values as depth, balance, care for detail, and presenting different angles objectively so that the reader decides. It says it holds publishing rights to several international titles (among them The New York Times and The Guardian), which explains the translated travel pieces credited to them. (https://aawsat.com/من-نحن)
- **Not found:** no published code of ethics, corrections policy, sponsorship rule or AI policy on aawsat.com's about page or on the parent group's about page (https://www.srmg.com/en/about).
- **Inferred (from travel pieces):** every piece is stamped with the exact publication time and both the Gregorian and Hijri date; every photo has its source in brackets. Advice is tied to a named person with a role and a company, never to "experts". (https://aawsat.com/يوميات-الشرق/سفر-وسياحة, https://aawsat.com/يوميات-الشرق/سفر-وسياحة/5303838-السفر-إلى-أوروبا-خلال-الصيف-إليك-ما-ينبغي-معرفته)
- **Inferred:** entry rules are scoped to a named group, not to "Arabs": a festival guide says visa-free entry applies to citizens of GCC states and eligible residents, and nothing broader. Event listings give the exact date, the edition number and the town for each item. (https://aawsat.com/يوميات-الشرق/سفر-وسياحة/5321821-دليلك-لمهرجانات-الخريف-في-أرمينيا)
- **Inferred:** prices are given in the venue's own currency, sometimes with a dollar equivalent; month names are written so every region understands them. (https://aawsat.com/يوميات-الشرق/سفر-وسياحة/5314005-ماذا-تزور-في-برشلونة؟)
- **Inferred:** a safety piece on travel scams ends each threat with a step the reader can take and check (open the official app or site yourself, call the number published there, not the one in the message), not with a vague warning. (https://aawsat.com/يوميات-الشرق/سفر-وسياحة/5324432-الوجه-الجديد-للاحتيال-في-السفر)

## 3. أراجيك (arageek.com): the product-list benchmark

**Why it is the benchmark.** A large general-interest Arabic magazine (tech, culture, education) with a dedicated "best of" list format for devices, and the only Arabic publisher in this search with a written content-development policy. Its policy is the closest Arabic model for our app-comparison and "best apps" articles. It is a benchmark for process and labelling, not a test lab.

**Editorial QC, in our words**
- **Stated:** submitted articles are reviewed by experienced editors in the relevant field; review covers fact-checking and fit with the magazine's policy, which keeps away from politics, religion, hate speech and copied content; proofreading follows; an article can go through several rounds of revision, rewriting or filling gaps. (https://www.arageek.com/content-policy, also on https://www.arageek.com/about)
- **Stated:** it refuses material already published elsewhere in whole or in part, and asks writers for knowledge of their subject, support from studies for their claims, and an original angle even when a topic has been covered elsewhere. (same pages)
- **Inferred (from a device list):** each list item has its own strengths and weaknesses, a short spec block with the availability date, and the article shows the author's name and a "last updated" date. Where a verdict is a summary of other outlets' reviews rather than the magazine's own, the page says so in a label. Ad slots are labelled «الإعلان». (https://www.arageek.com/tech/أفضل-أجهزة-تابلت-لطلاب-الطب-معايير-ومو)
- **Not found:** no test method, scoring formula, corrections page or AI policy.

## Checked, not adopted

- **البوابة التقنية (aitnews.com)** and **عالم التقنية (tech-wd.com)**, the long-running Arabic tech news sites: their about pages describe coverage and history, not standards; AIT News's about page lists being a marketing platform for tech companies among its aims. The comparison piece read summarises an outside report and names details it lacked; no test method is stated anywhere. Not a usable review benchmark. (https://aitnews.com/about-us/, https://aitnews.com/2026/01/29/مراجعة-مقارنة-oneplus-15-و15r/, https://www.tech-wd.com/wd/about/, https://www.tech-wd.com/wd/policy/)
- **Condé Nast Traveller Middle East (cntraveller.me)**: the site is in English (page language en-AE) with no Arabic content found, so it is outside this market's language. (https://www.cntravellerme.com/)
- **مجلة العربي**, named in `SKILL.md` as a tone standard, was not checked this session. Web search was unavailable during this research, so candidates were reached only by direct visits to known sites; the owner may want to add a section after a search.

## Editor's lens: the questions to ask of every Arabic article

Ask these after the line edit, in one pass. Each one comes from a standard above, adapted to what we can honestly do.

1. **The date test («آخر تحديث»).** Would every competitor fact still be true if a reader opened the app's store page in their country today (`apps.apple.com/sa/`, `/ae/`, `/eg/`)? If you did not check it today, it goes or turns generic. If a fact changed, is `updated` today?
2. **Words that age («قريباً»، «جديد»، «هذا العام»، «حالياً»).** Is every time-bound word either checked today or removed? A dated page with a stale "soon" misleads more than an undated one.
3. **The basis of every judgement (أساس الحكم).** Does a comparison say in one sentence what we did: read each app's own pages and store listing, and that we make VoyageLog? Is there any «جرّبنا»، «اختبرنا»، «استخدمناه في رحلاتنا» about a competitor? We did not, so it goes.
4. **Disclosure (الإفصاح).** Is «نحن نصنع VoyageLog» in the first or second paragraph of every comparison and alternatives piece, and does nothing later read like an advertorial (a «إعلان» with no label)?
5. **Justified selection (مبرر الاختيار).** Does every app in a list carry one concrete reason it is there and one honest limit (ميزة وعيب)? Would the list still make sense if VoyageLog were not in it?
6. **Same criteria for all (معايير موحدة).** Are the criteria (account required, offline, export, Arabic interface, price model, platforms) stated before the verdicts, and is every app judged on the same ones?
7. **Limits of the comparison (حدود المقارنة).** Does the piece say what it could not confirm (prices that differ by country, an Arabic interface we did not see on the storefront)?
8. **VoyageLog's own limits.** Is the "when VoyageLog is the wrong choice" section specific (route tracking, trip planning, cloud sync, web or iPad), so a reader who needs those leaves with a better answer?
9. **Scoped rules (نطاق القاعدة).** Is every visa, entry, roaming or customs statement tied to a named nationality and checked on the official source today? If not, does it use the generic «تحقق من شروط التأشيرة لجواز سفرك» line?
10. **Dated practical facts.** Is every event date, season, opening month or festival edition verified for this year on the organiser's or tourism body's own site? If not, is it written as a month range or cut?
11. **Currency.** Is any price in the venue country's own currency, written out («درهم مغربي»), verified, and given without a converted equivalent that will age with the exchange rate?
12. **No anonymous authority («يقول الخبراء»، «تشير الدراسات»).** The benchmarks name their sources; we cannot quote or cite. So every such phrase becomes a plain verified fact or goes. Vendor promises we cannot test are framed as the vendor's own claim.
13. **A step the reader can check.** Does privacy or safety advice end in a concrete action («صدّر نسخة احتياطية بصيغة JSON واحفظها خارج الهاتف») rather than «احرص على خصوصيتك»?
14. **Reader questions as structure (أسئلة القارئ).** Do the H2s follow the questions this reader would ask, in order, without turning every heading into a question?
15. **Original angle (أصالة الزاوية).** Does the article give something no store page does: how to choose, a sample note from جبل عمّان or خان الخليلي, a decision the reader can make tonight? Is nothing in it lifted, translated or closely paraphrased from another site?
16. **Translated or local («مترجم أم مكتوب بالعربية»).** Does any passage read as translated from English (the calques in `SKILL.md`)? Benchmarks label translation openly; our articles must not need the label.
17. **Neutral ground (الحياد).** Does the article avoid any political or religious position, keep borders and country counts neutral and verified, and treat religious sites only as respectful moments, never as something to rate?
18. **Corrections readiness (تنويه وتصويب).** Could the editorial report show, for every fact, where and when it was checked? A fact that would need a public correction if wrong and has no source in the report is not ready. Corrections are made in place with `updated` set and logged in `docs/editorial/ar.md`.
19. **The small errors that get corrected publicly.** In the final proofreading pass (المراجعة والتدقيق): headline spelling, gender and number agreement, and any «يمين / يسار» or «أعلى اليمين» describing the app screen. The interface mirrors in RTL, so a position word copied from an English draft is likely wrong; describe the control by its Arabic label from `src/locales/ar.js` instead.
20. **Plural voice (صوت «نحن»).** Is the voice «نحن» throughout, article and FAQ, with no single expert persona, no claimed field expertise and no invented credentials?

## What we never borrow

- Their text, headlines, standfirsts, section names or list titles, in Arabic or translated back.
- Their rankings, list selections, "best" picks, ratings, scores or the order of their items.
- Their signature formats: Q-and-A service layouts copied wholesale, «تقييم» boxes, spec blocks, event lines in their date | place pattern, dual Gregorian-Hijri stamps, corrections-page layout.
- Recognisable phrasing, slogans or taglines, including how they describe themselves.
- Their names: never mention, quote, compare ourselves to or link ناشيونال جيوغرافيك العربية, الشرق الأوسط, أراجيك or any other benchmark inside an article.
- Claims we cannot make: travel on the ground, testing devices or apps, expert sources, an advisory board, a named author.
