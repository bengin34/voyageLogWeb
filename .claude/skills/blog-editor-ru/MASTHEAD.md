# Masthead lens: Russian-language market

The quality bar for the Russian desk, taken from how respected Russian-language travel, service and tech publications describe and show their own editorial work. It is a set of standards, never a source of content (reference, section 10). Researched on 2026-10-24 by reading the pages listed below directly (the session's web-search budget was exhausted, so candidates were checked by their known domains). Everything below is paraphrased; quotes are a few words at most. Each claim is marked **stated** (the publisher says it on the page listed) or **inferred** (read from their published work, not from a written policy). Re-check the URLs before relying on a detail.

The lens is about editorial craft only: how facts are checked, dated, attributed and limited. It takes no view on any publication's ownership, politics or coverage choices.

## 1. «Вокруг света» (vokrugsveta.ru)

**Why it is the benchmark.** The oldest Russian-language travel and geography magazine (in print since 1861, online since 2006) and still active, with a daily travel section. For most Russian readers it is the name that means careful, explanatory writing about places.

**Editorial QC, in our words**
- **Stated:** the site names its editorial director and the editor-in-chief of the site, and gives a single editorial contact. (https://www.vokrugsveta.ru/company/)
- **Stated:** where the site reprints someone else's material, the exception is flagged in the text it concerns; agency copy and photo rights are credited to their owners. (https://www.vokrugsveta.ru/company/republish/)
- **Stated, and a caution for us:** the advertising page offers sponsored special projects integrated into editorial content; no page read describes how such material is labelled or kept apart from editorial. (https://www.vokrugsveta.ru/pages/advert/)
- **Not found:** no published editorial standards, fact-checking, corrections or AI policy on the pages read.
- **Inferred (from a travel piece of 22 October 2026, on Totma):** a dated piece with a named author; a one-line standfirst that says what the place is known for; a numbered list where every reason rests on a checkable fact (a date, a named building, a named museum) rather than an adjective; every photo credited; and a closing "what to know before you go" box covering how to get there, how long to stay and what to see, written in durable terms (route via a hub city, one day or two) with no prices or timetables that would date. (https://www.vokrugsveta.ru/travels/okno-v-kaliforniyu-na-russkom-severe-5-prichin-zaglyanut-v-totmu-khotya-by-raz-id76713328/)

## 2. Т—Ж, travel desk (t-j.ru, the T-Bank journal)

**Why it is the benchmark.** The largest Russian-language service-journalism title, with a dedicated travel desk (guides, routes, entry rules, trip-cost diaries). Its pages describe its own process in unusual detail, and it is a brand medium owned by a company that sells products, which is close to our situation: we write about a category in which we sell an app.

**Editorial QC, in our words**
- **Stated:** the owner's money funds the journal, but the owner does not commission editorial pieces or set the agenda; promotional pieces about the group's products always carry a «Партнерский материал» tag and are not disguised as editorial. When something influenced a product choice, the piece says so at the top. (https://t-j.ru/about/)
- **Stated:** when they use another company's data, they name the data provider. Prices shown in product cards update automatically and may differ from those in the text, and the page says so. In the shopping section they offer to correct factual errors promptly. (https://t-j.ru/about/)
- **Stated:** no ghostwriters, agents or marketers writing on an author's behalf; each author writes and edits with an editor. Copying is grounds for ending the relationship. A text assembled from forums and passed off as personal experience or expert interviews is treated as deception. (https://t-j.ru/about/)
- **Stated:** there are two tiers, and they are kept apart. Reader posts share experience and are not asked for sources. Paid editorial pieces require expertise, homework and evidence (the page's word is «вещдоки»). (https://t-j.ru/manual/)
- **Stated (travel desk guide):** the desk describes its fact-check as «жесточайший фактчек»: every fact, figure and legal rule a piece relies on is checked; for news about other countries they read that country's laws and primary sources in other languages rather than reprinting agency copy. Guides must be dense with specifics (advice, prices) and still readable. No draft is published without at least one round of editor questions and revisions. (https://t-j.ru/how-to-write-for-travel/)
- **Inferred (from an entry-rules service piece):** the byline states the author's method in a few words («изучила правила въезда», she studied the entry rules), not a claim of having been there; requirements are attributed to the official source that sets them (embassy, foreign ministry); where an official source could not confirm something, the text says so plainly and then gives the usual rule; where recent reader reports could not be found, it says that too. In the comments, an editor answered a reader by giving the exact dates on which the consulate had been phoned. The title carries the year, and a reader asked why the comments were older than the year in the title: a visible cost of refreshing a year without making the update obvious. (https://t-j.ru/romania-entry-rules/)
- **Inferred (from a product round-up in the comparison series):** the piece opens by saying what the list is based on, then a «Как я выбирал» section lists the selection criteria before any product; items are grouped by use (budget, without a screen, for athletes), and each item's limits (subscription only, no GPS) sit next to its strengths. (https://t-j.ru/best-fitness-trackers/)
- **Not found:** no AI-content policy and no formal corrections page on the pages read (there is a "report a problem" form in the footer).

## 3. «Афиша Daily» (daily.afisha.ru), city and travel section

**Why it is the benchmark.** The successor to the «Афиша» magazine (since 1999) and the city-guide voice many Russian-speaking readers grew up with: practical, opinionated, about where to go and what to do. Its travel pieces sit in the «Город» section.

**Editorial QC, in our words**
- **Stated:** a short editorial policy page commits to verified information, no plagiarism or falsification, several points of view and opinions from experts independent of one another, and says reviews (рецензирование) happen only by prior formal agreement with the editors. The page is brief and does not describe a process behind these commitments. (https://daily.afisha.ru/legal/redakcionnaya_politika/)
- **Stated:** the about page names the editor-in-chief and lists the commercial special-projects team separately from the editorial staff. (https://daily.afisha.ru/about/)
- **Stated (site feature):** readers can report a typo or error by selecting the text and pressing Ctrl+Enter. (same pages)
- **Not found:** no fact-checking procedure, corrections policy or AI policy on the pages read.
- **Inferred (from a winter-trips list, October 2026):** every destination gets a reason tied to the season (water temperature, low season, fewer crowds) and a separate "how to get there" block; where a schedule is quoted, the validity window is given as a date. A lesson we draw: in that sample every transport block cites the same airline group's routes; whatever the reason, a practical block that keeps naming one provider reads as a partnership to the reader, so we name options generically or not at all. (https://daily.afisha.ru/cities/37963-v-samarkand-za-kovrom-v-guanchzhou-za-chaem-8-nebanalnyh-idey-dlya-zimnih-poezdok/)

## 4. iXBT.com: the product and tech-review benchmark

**Why it is the benchmark.** The longest-running Russian-language tech publication (since 1997), known for long instrumented reviews. It is the closest model for how our app-comparison articles should separate claims from checks.

**Editorial QC, in our words**
- **Stated:** a firm position that the site never publishes commissioned articles; staff and freelance authors are said to have a technical higher education. An unsigned article is prepared by the site's collective of authors, which is how our plural byline works too. Editorial mail is answered within ten working days. (https://www.ixbt.com/doc/about.html)
- **Stated:** comment moderation uses a neural-network system. That is the only AI mention found; there is no AI-content policy for articles. (same page)
- **Inferred (from a smartphone review, October 2026):** the manufacturer's promises are attributed as such («по уверению разработчиков», as the developers assure us); measured values are marked as the site's own («согласно нашим измерениям») and set against the declared value when they differ; test conditions are stated (brightness level, power-saving off) and the results are called best-case figures that real use will likely not reach; comparison tables use the same benchmark versions for every device; the verdict names who the device suits and what it lacks. (https://www.ixbt.com/mobile/honor-x9e-pro-review.html)
- **Not found:** no corrections policy; no standalone methodology page was located without search.

## Editor's lens: the questions to ask of every Russian article

Ask these after the line edit, in one pass. Each comes from a standard above, adapted to what we can honestly do.

1. **Primary source for every competitor fact.** Was each claim about another app (free tier, account, offline, export, platforms, Russian in the interface) checked today on that app's own page or store listing, the way Т—Ж checks every fact a piece relies on? If not, it goes or turns generic.
2. **Which country's store.** The reader may be in Kazakhstan, Georgia, Serbia, Israel or Germany. Does any price, availability or payment statement say which store or country it comes from, or does it stay neutral? Т—Ж writes for one country's passport holders; we do not, so we never assume one.
3. **Date or durability.** Is every fact that can change either verified today (and `updated` set to today) or written in durable terms, the way «Вокруг света» keeps its trip box free of timetables? A year in the title means the body was re-checked this year, not just the title.
4. **Method in one line.** Does a comparison or alternatives piece say plainly what we did: read each app's own pages and store listing, and we make VoyageLog? Nothing more.
5. **No borrowed experience.** Is there any sentence implying we took a competitor app on a trip, used it for weeks, or tested it on a phone? Т—Ж calls research passed off as experience deception; we did not travel with these apps, so the sentence goes.
6. **Claimed versus checked.** Is every vendor promise we could not check framed as the vendor's own claim («по словам разработчиков», «в описании приложения»), the way iXBT separates declared from measured?
7. **Disclosure of interest.** Is «VoyageLog делаем мы» in the first or second paragraph of every comparison, alternatives and best-of piece, and does nothing later read like a partner piece in disguise?
8. **Criteria before verdicts.** In a list or comparison, are the selection criteria stated before the first app (account, offline, export, price model, platforms), and is every app judged on the same ones, as in a «Как я выбирал» opening?
9. **One reason and one limit per item.** Does each app in a list carry one concrete reason it is there and one honest limit? An item with only praise is an advert.
10. **Would the list survive without us?** Is every app there because a reader in this market would actually consider it, not to pad the count or to make VoyageLog look good by comparison?
11. **Say what the article cannot confirm.** Where we could not verify something (a price that varies by country, a feature hidden behind sign-up), does the text say that once, plainly, instead of guessing?
12. **VoyageLog's own limits.** Is the "when VoyageLog is the wrong choice" section specific (no route tracking, no trip planning, no cloud sync, no web or iPad version claimed), and does it tell that reader what kind of app to look for instead?
13. **Practical information.** Seasons, opening months, distances: is each on an official source, or stated as a range? Does any practical block keep naming one airline, bank or service where a generic phrase would do?
14. **Reasons rest on facts.** Does every "why go" or "why use" line rest on something checkable (a named place, a date, a feature in `en.js`) rather than an adjective?
15. **Sourcing without citations.** Is every verified fact stated plainly in the text, with the source URL only in `docs/editorial/ru.md`? No links, no «по данным…» lines, no named outlets.
16. **Correction readiness.** If a reader wrote in tomorrow, could the report show where and when each fact was checked, the way a Т—Ж editor could name the day the consulate was phoned? If not, the fact is not ready to publish.
17. **Re-check what was kept.** If the article was edited before, did you re-verify the facts you kept, not only the ones you changed?
18. **Plural voice, no persona.** Is the voice «мы» throughout, with no invented single author, credentials or travel biography? An unsigned piece is the collective's, as on iXBT; it must still be checkable.

## What we never borrow

- Their text, headlines, standfirsts, list titles, section headings or box titles, in Russian or translated.
- Their rankings, list selections, destination picks, "best of" choices, badges, emoji markers or scores.
- Their signature formats: Т—Ж's diary and cost-diary formats, reader-questionnaire series and partner layouts; «Вокруг света»'s numbered "reasons to go" format with its closing trip box; «Афиша»'s city-guide set pieces; iXBT's specification tables and benchmark charts. We may share a generic practice (a short practical block, criteria before a list) but never their layout, wording or series names.
- Recognisable phrasing, slogans and self-descriptions, including their lines about independence or fact-checking.
- Their names: never mention, quote, compare ourselves to or link «Вокруг света», Т—Ж, «Афиша», iXBT or any other benchmark inside an article.
- Claims we cannot make: on-the-ground checks, phone calls to officials, lab measurements, testing on devices, a named expert author.
