# Masthead lens: French-language market

The quality bar for the French desk, taken from how the most trusted French-language travel and consumer-testing publishers say they check their own work. It is a set of standards, never a source of content (reference, section 10). Researched on 2026-10-24. Everything below is paraphrased; quotes are a few words at most. Each claim is marked **stated** (the publisher says it on the page listed) or **inferred** (read from their published work or general practice, not from a written policy). Re-check the URLs before relying on a detail.

## 1. Le Guide du Routard (Hachette; routard.com)

**Why it is the benchmark.** The reference French guidebook for independent travellers for decades, the one most French readers have owned. Its authority rests on a simple promise: places are in the guide because someone visited them as an ordinary customer, and nobody paid to be there.

**Editorial QC, in our words**
- **Stated:** researchers travel unannounced and identify themselves only when that is the sole way to get a piece of information. Thousands of addresses are re-checked anonymously every year. (https://www.routard.com/fr/pages/le-guide-du-routard, https://www.routard.com/fr/pages/faq)
- **Stated:** no establishment can buy a place in the guide; the guide says it has never taken money from hoteliers or restaurateurs. Flights and car hire are paid by the publisher and meals, taxis and rooms are reimbursed, so authors have no reason to ask for anything free. (FAQ)
- **Stated:** about 95 % of titles are revised every year, a few less-requested ones every two years, and the guide admits that rarely visited regions are not always fully revised. That admission is the point: the revision cycle is stated, including where it falls short. (FAQ)
- **Stated:** an establishment can submit itself, but it gets in only if an author checks it on a later trip. Reader tips arrive by post, email and forum and are acknowledged. (FAQ)
- **Stated:** "good value" is defined as the best experience for the price, not the cheapest, and addresses are sorted into named price bands rather than exact prices. (Le Guide du Routard page)
- **Stated:** routard.com calls itself editorially independent; its about page also lists advertising and partnership roles, so the separation of commerce and editorial is asserted, not described. (https://www.routard.com/fr/pages/qui-sommes-nous)
- **Inferred (from the guides):** practical details are given in a fixed, scannable order (how to get there, opening times, price band), and a selection is justified by one concrete reason, not by an adjective.

## 2. Lonely Planet, French edition (Edi8 / Editis; lonelyplanet.fr)

**Why it is the benchmark.** The other guidebook every French-speaking traveller knows, published in French since 1993 and strongest on long, independent trips. It is the closest benchmark for our readers' trips (multi-city, abroad) and for a structured production chain.

**Editorial QC, in our words**
- **Stated:** guides carry no advertising, and authors may not accept free rooms or meals in exchange for favourable coverage. (https://www.lonelyplanet.fr/lesprit-de-nos-guides)
- **Stated:** authors prepare before leaving (news, trends, what travellers expect), then on the ground re-check every detail of the previous edition and add what is new. (https://www.lonelyplanet.fr/comment-fait-un-guide)
- **Stated:** the chain after the trip is explicit: the author checks before sending, an editor goes through the whole manuscript and queries the author on specific points, cartographers check the maps, a proofreader passes before print. Reader letters are read by editors; outside experts write the context boxes. (same page)
- **Stated:** the French edition follows the English original by about four months, which the publisher says openly. Lag between the source and the localized text is acknowledged, not hidden. (same page)
- **Stated:** in 2007 the BBC's purchase came with a commitment to editorial independence. (https://www.lonelyplanet.fr/lhistoire-de-lonely-planet)
- **Not found:** no corrections process, dating rule or AI policy on the pages read.

## 3. UFC-Que Choisir (quechoisir.org): the product-review benchmark

**Why it is the benchmark.** The French consumer association whose comparative tests are the national reference for "which one should I buy". Its method pages are the model for any article that compares products, including our app comparisons.

**Editorial QC, in our words**
- **Stated:** products are bought anonymously like any consumer would, tested by the association's engineers with independent labs, under a strict protocol covering performance, ergonomics and safety; tests follow the French comparative-testing standard NF X50-005; more than half of tests in 2025 were run with European partners (ICRT). Topics come from what consumers need and from current events. (https://www.quechoisir.org/page-qce-notre-methodologie-n175546/)
- **Stated:** journalists follow the Munich Charter and the French journalists' code of ethics. (same page)
- **Stated:** the association says it depends neither on advertising nor on subsidies from manufacturers or retailers, and can refuse donations that would compromise independence. It does license its logo to products rated above 14/20, and says so on the funding page. (https://www.quechoisir.org/page-qce-notre-financement-n175550/)
- **Stated:** every product category has its own dated, public protocol page under « Comment nous testons » listing the criteria. (https://www.quechoisir.org/rub-comment-nous-testons-t617/)
- **Stated (smartphone protocol, 8 October 2026):** nine named criteria; ease of use judged by lab experts, not a user panel, and the page says so; software updates and the date of the latest security patch are re-checked every two months, and an outdated system is penalised. The test admits what it does not cover (apps other than the browser). (https://www.quechoisir.org/comment-nous-testons-smartphones-protocole-n15157/)
- **Not found:** no published weighting formula, corrections policy or AI policy on the pages read.

## Editor's lens: the questions to ask of every French article

Ask these after the line edit, in one pass. Each one comes from a standard above, adapted to what we can honestly do.

1. **The date test.** Would every competitor fact in the piece still be true if a reader checked the app's French store page today? If you did not check it today, it goes or turns generic (Que Choisir dates and re-checks its protocols; we re-check at each edit).
2. **The `updated` field.** If a fact changed, is `updated` today? Does the article avoid saying "currently" about anything it does not re-check (Routard states its revision cycle and its gaps)?
3. **Method in one sentence.** Does a comparison article say plainly what we did: read each app's own pages and store listing on a given date, and that we make VoyageLog? Nothing more, nothing less.
4. **No borrowed fieldwork.** Is there any sentence implying we travelled with, tested over weeks, or "used on a trip" a competitor app? Routard and Lonely Planet earn that claim by going there; we did not, so it goes.
5. **Who judged.** Where the article gives an opinion (easiest, most private), is it framed as our judgement from the apps' published features, the way Que Choisir says experts, not users, judged ease of use?
6. **Disclosure of interest.** Is « Nous éditons VoyageLog » in the first or second paragraph of every comparison and alternatives piece, and does nothing later read like an advert for it?
7. **Nobody buys a place.** Is every app in a list there for a reason a reader can see (a feature, a price model, a platform), never because it is famous or to pad the count? Would we keep the list if VoyageLog were not in it?
8. **Justified selection.** Does each list item carry one concrete reason it is there, and one honest limit? An item with only praise is an advert.
9. **Criteria before verdicts.** In a comparison, are the criteria stated before the apps (account required, offline, export, price model, platforms), and is every app judged on the same ones?
10. **Stated limits of the comparison.** Does the piece say what it does not cover (prices that vary by country, features we could not confirm), as Que Choisir names what a protocol leaves out?
11. **VoyageLog's own limits.** Is the "when VoyageLog is the wrong choice" section specific (route tracking, planning, cloud sync, iPad/web), so a reader who needs those leaves with a better answer?
12. **Practical information that is dated by nature.** Prices, seasons, opening months, roaming rules: is each verified on the official source for the right country (France, Belgium, Switzerland, Québec), or written as a range or band instead of a number?
13. **Market fit.** Is every app, service or photo-book provider named available and relevant to a francophone reader, with no French-only assumption for Belgian, Swiss or Québec readers?
14. **Re-check the previous edition.** If the article was edited before, did you re-verify the facts that were kept, not only the new ones (Lonely Planet's authors re-check the previous edition on the ground)?
15. **Sourcing without citations.** Is every verified fact stated plainly in the text, with the source URL in the editorial report only? Is any vendor promise we cannot test labelled as the vendor's own claim?
16. **Context a reader cannot get from a store page.** Does the article add something the apps' own pages do not: how to choose, what to write, a real example note from a francophone city?
17. **Plural voice.** Is the byline voice « nous » throughout, with no single "expert author" persona and no invented credentials?
18. **Correction readiness.** If a reader wrote in to say a fact is wrong, could the report for this article show where each fact was checked? If not, the fact is not ready to publish.

## What we never borrow

- Their text, headlines, standfirsts, list titles or section headings, in French or translated.
- Their rankings, list selections, "best of" choices, award labels, stickers, scores or price-band names.
- Their signature formats: guide-style address blocks, their symbol systems, test-score tables, star or « coup de cœur » devices, protocol layouts.
- Recognisable phrasing or slogans, including taglines about independence.
- Their names: never mention, quote, compare ourselves to or link Le Routard, Lonely Planet, Que Choisir or any other benchmark inside an article.
- Claims we cannot make: anonymous inspection, lab testing, on-the-ground verification, a named expert author.
