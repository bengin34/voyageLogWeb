# Masthead lens: Dutch desk (nl)

This file is the quality bar for the Dutch desk: how the most trusted Dutch-language travel and consumer publications check their own work, turned into questions the editor asks of every VoyageLog article. It is not a source of content, wording or facts. Rules for using it: `.claude/skills/blog-editor-core/REFERENCE.md`, section 10.

**When:** a separate pass after the line edit (see `SKILL.md`, workflow). Read the article once more with the Editor's lens below, fix what falls short in place, and mention the lens fixes under "What changed" in the report.

**Researched:** 24 October 2026, from the pages listed below. Re-check the URLs before relying on a detail; these organisations update their pages.

**Labels used below**
- [stated]: written policy or method on the page cited.
- [observed]: seen in their published work on the page cited; a practice we infer, not a written policy.
- [not verified]: background knowledge that could not be checked on a primary page for this file.

**Research limits.** Web search was unavailable during research, so pages were fetched directly. The sites of NRC and de Volkskrant refused automated fetching, De Standaard and Op Pad returned 403, and Columbus Travel sends visitors to a login wall. Their own newsroom codes, press-trip rules and correction pages were therefore not read. Nothing below attributes a practice to them beyond what is marked.

## 1. ANWB: Kampioen and the travel-information pages

**Why it is the benchmark.** The ANWB is the Dutch members' association for mobility and holidays and the default source Dutch travellers consult for practical rules abroad (toll, vignettes, driving rules, camping). Its member magazine Kampioen appears four times a year with a print run the ANWB puts at 3.7 million copies, which makes it one of the most read publications in the country. Its practical pages are what a Dutch reader compares any travel service piece against. (Flanders has its own counterparts, such as VAB; they were not researched.)

**Editorial QC, in our words**
- **Sign-off on every practical page** [stated on the page]: the country information pages end with a line naming the in-house holiday expert who compiled and checked the information, the date of the last update, and a plain warning that rules may have changed since. Seen on the Austria toll page, last updated 4 June 2026.
- **Questions first** [observed]: the same page is organised around the questions a driver actually has (where you pay, what it costs, what to sort out before leaving, where the sticker goes) and answers each one directly under its heading.
- **The operator has the final word** [observed]: for details that only the operator can settle, the page sends the reader to the toll operator's own site instead of restating everything.
- **Named, qualified experts** [stated]: a public page introduces the experts by name, background and field, so the reader knows who stands behind advice on cars, bikes, camping or holidays.
- **Product tests** [stated]: every product in a test goes through the same procedure; measurable criteria come first, a panel or experts judge what a lab cannot measure (ease of use), and the starting point is real-world use. Manufacturers see their results and specifications before publication and may point out factual errors; a valid objection leads to a retest of that part. When ANWB's own brands are in a test, an independent expert oversees the procedure. The page does not publish how criteria are weighted.
- **Advertising labelled** [observed]: paid pieces on the Kampioen pages carry an "advertorial" label, and offers carry their validity period.
- **Not found:** a published redactiestatuut, corrections policy or AI policy for Kampioen or the travel pages.

**Pages used**
- https://www.anwb.nl/kampioen (magazine overview, advertorial labels, validity periods)
- https://www.anwb.nl/kampioen/algemeen/contact (frequency, print run)
- https://www.anwb.nl/vakantie/oostenrijk/informatie/tol (sign-off line, question structure, referral to the operator)
- https://www.anwb.nl/lidmaatschap/experts (named experts)
- https://www.anwb.nl/lidmaatschap/testen/verantwoording (test accountability)

## 2. Consumentenbond: the product-review benchmark

**Why it is the benchmark.** For "which one should I pick" questions, Dutch readers trust the Consumentenbond more than any review site; its labels are the shorthand for a fair comparison. It tests more than 2,000 products and services a year, pays for almost all of that research itself and never tests on commission from a manufacturer. Our app comparisons (best apps, Polarsteps alternatives) are read by people who know this standard, even if they never think of it by name.

**Editorial QC, in our words** (all [stated])
- **Selection is explained, including what is left out.** Market researchers choose what to test from sales figures, market share and members' interest. The method page lists the kinds of products they skip (tiny market share, poor availability, models that change during the test, niche brands) and admits a test may cover only one price range. The smartphone method page has a section on how complete the test is, naming which brands are in scope and why.
- **Same test for everyone.** A fixed test programme describes exactly how each aspect is measured, under the same conditions, so results are comparable.
- **Judge the job, not the looks.** They test how well a product does what it is for, not how attractive it is.
- **Reviews do not count.** User reviews and star ratings do not enter the score, because the conditions behind them are unknown. A wave of reviews that contradicts a result is a reason to re-examine the method, not to change the verdict.
- **Buy like a consumer.** Wherever possible they buy products in the shop, so a maker cannot hand over a tuned sample. Loans are the stated exception for very expensive items.
- **Verdicts expire.** A label lapses when a newer test produces a better product; a label shown in advertising carries the month of the last test update; prices and availability of tested models are rechecked; models that stay on sale unchanged for a long time are retested to see if the verdict still holds.
- **Contact with makers is factual.** Makers hear only verifiable measurements about their own product, can pass on corrections, and are asked for a reaction where relevant.
- **Scores are broken down.** The overall mark is built from part scores, so the reader sees where a product is strong and where it falls short; the method page says who pays for the test.
- **Independence** is framed as choosing topics, conclusions and advice without influence from business or government.
- **Not found:** an AI policy or a general corrections page.

**Pages used**
- https://www.consumentenbond.nl/over-ons/wie-zijn-we/onderzoek (research method; content sits in expandable sections)
- https://www.consumentenbond.nl/smartphone/hoe-wij-testen (an example method page for one category)
- https://www.consumentenbond.nl/over-ons/wie-zijn-we/logo-beste-koop-en-beste-uit-de-test (labels, expiry, dating)
- https://www.consumentenbond.nl/over-ons/wie-zijn-we/onafhankelijkheid-consumentenbond (independence)

## 3. The quality-paper travel desks and the press-council codes

**Why it is the benchmark.** `SKILL.md` sets the eindredactie of NRC, de Volkskrant and De Standaard as the standard for our prose and fairness. Their own codes could not be read (see research limits). That these papers work within the press-council system of their country (Raad voor de Journalistiek in the Netherlands, its Flemish namesake in Belgium) is [not verified] here. What follows is the norm those councils publish, which is the floor any serious Dutch-language newsroom is measured against.

**Netherlands: Leidraad of the Raad voor de Journalistiek (version September 2026)** [stated]
- Report truthfully, verifiably and as completely as possible; avoid one-sided, tendentious coverage.
- Be open about the assignment, approach and method (added on 1 September 2026, explicitly as a basis of credibility).
- Work independently and avoid even the appearance of a conflict of interest.
- Keep facts, claims and opinions clearly apart; reviews may be opinionated, and are recognisable as opinion.
- Name sources in principle; weigh whether a link serves the reader.
- Correct errors and culpable omissions promptly and in a fitting way.
- Do not use images out of context; disclose manipulation that changes an image.

**Flanders: Code van de Raad voor de Journalistiek** [stated]
- Publish only information whose origin you know; verify it; speed never beats accuracy.
- Correct inaccurate facts loyally. For serious errors online, quietly updating or deleting is not enough: acknowledge the error and publish the correction.
- Journalism is paid from the newsroom's own budget; when a third party exceptionally covers costs (a press trip), the newsroom safeguards independence and is transparent where credibility is at stake.
- Advertising must be clearly recognisable as not journalism.
- AI guideline: editorial choices inside automated processes must meet the code, final responsibility stays with the editor-in-chief, and the reader is told when content is wholly or partly produced by automated systems.
- Plagiarism guideline: no substantial takeover of another medium's work, and attribution does not excuse it; a summary must differ in overall impression and add its own work; feeding systematically on other newsrooms' work is not acceptable.

**Pages used**
- https://rvdj.nl/leidraad/ (history of the Leidraad, principles)
- https://rvdj.nl/leidraad/sept-2026/ (full text, September 2026)
- https://www.rvdj.be/pagina/journalistieke-code (about the Flemish code)
- https://www.rvdj.be/code-raad-voor-de-journalistiek (articles and guidelines, including the AI and plagiarism guidelines)

## Editor's lens: questions for every VoyageLog article

Ask each of these after the line edit. A "no" is fixed in the article (or in the report where the article cannot carry it). Where a question collides with a house rule (no links, no citations, plural byline), the house rule wins and the practice becomes internal verification.

1. **Answer first.** Do the first two sentences answer the query and say which choice fits which reader, the way a good practical page answers under its own heading?
2. **The sign-off test.** For every practical or competitor fact: could we name, in the report, the primary page it was checked on and the date? If not, it goes generic or out. The article carries no sources; the report carries all of them.
3. **Honest dating.** Does `updated` reflect a real check today? Is anything a reader will act on (free tier, subscription model, platform availability, season dates) either verified today or written so it cannot go stale?
4. **Change warning, once.** Where a reader might pay or rely on a rule, is there one plain sentence that it can change and where to look (by name, without a link: "in de App Store")? Not a hedge on every line.
5. **Why these apps.** Does a comparison say in one sentence why these apps and not others (what Dutch and Flemish readers actually use or search for), and admit what is left out?
6. **Same questions for every app.** Is every app measured on the same axes (account needed, offline, free tier, export, route tracking, platforms), with comparable cells in a table and no axis that only flatters us?
7. **Judge the job.** Does the recommendation rest on what the reader wants done (a journal they will reread, a route on a map, a printed book) rather than on design, popularity, store ratings or review counts?
8. **No test we did not do.** Is every trace of hands-on testing gone: "getest", "in onze test", "we probeerden onderweg", lab or panel vocabulary, scores, rapportcijfers, test-winner labels? We describe documented features; we did not field-test.
9. **Fact, claim, opinion.** Is each sentence about a competitor a verified fact stated plainly, and is our judgement recognisable as ours ("wij zouden ... kiezen")? A maker's marketing line never appears as a fact or as "volgens de makers".
10. **The maker's read.** Could the people behind Polarsteps, Journi or Day One read their paragraph and find no factual error and no unfair framing? Fairness to the app we compete with is the strongest proof of independence we can give.
11. **Interest declared.** Is the disclosure that we make VoyageLog stated once, early, plainly, and does nothing later quietly undercut it (praise of our app in the neutral sections, competitors described only by their weak points)?
12. **Limits next to strengths.** Are VoyageLog's limits (no route tracking, no cloud sync, no web, no sharing) stated where the reader would otherwise assume them, and is the "wrong choice" case as concrete as the case for us?
13. **Earned superlatives.** Is every "beste", "enige", "meest" backed by a criterion stated in the piece, and is a numbered list either justified as a ranking or clearly not one?
14. **What would flip the verdict.** If one competitor fact changed (a free tier, an offline mode), would a recommendation flip? Then that fact is listed in the report as the first thing to recheck.
15. **Illustrations are illustrations.** Are example notes, trips and cities framed as examples ("een notitie als ..."), never told as something that happened to us or to a named person? The plural byline cannot have been to Gent last week.
16. **Commerce in its place.** Is the `{{cta}}` the only sales moment, are Pro features described as facts without upsell language, and is no comparison section written as an advert?
17. **Both sides of the border.** Does each practical point hold in Flanders as well as the Netherlands (store, terminology, providers), or is it marked as NL-only or BE-only?
18. **Original in impression.** Compared with the pages that rank for this query (seen during fact-checking), does our article differ in structure, selection and wording, not only in sentences? Store descriptions are paraphrased, never carried over.
19. **Corrections are visible to the owner.** If the edit fixes a factual error in a published version, does the report name it as a correction under "Still uncertain, for the owner", so the owner can decide whether readers should be told?

## What we never borrow

- **Their content:** text, intros, headlines, FAQ questions, tables, examples, list selections, rankings, scores and verdicts. Our selection of apps and cities is our own, made for our reader.
- **Their signature formats and labels:** test-winner and best-buy predicates (these are licensed marks), "Afrader"-style warnings, rapportcijfers and part scores, a "hoe wij testen" box, a named-expert sign-off line on the page, Kampioen's series titles and section names.
- **Recognisable phrasing:** their headline patterns, their standard warning lines, their FAQ wording. If a sentence of ours sounds like it came from an ANWB or Consumentenbond page, rewrite it.
- **Their names in our articles:** no mention, quote, link or appeal to authority ("de Consumentenbond adviseert", "volgens de ANWB"), not even as a comparison.
- **Their facts as our source:** an ANWB, Consumentenbond or newspaper page is not a primary source for a competitor app, a price or a rule abroad. Check the app's own pages, the Dutch or Belgian store page, or the operator.
- **Practices we cannot honestly follow:** lab tests, panels, buying products anonymously, on-the-ground reporting, named experts, retest cycles. We keep the standard behind them (comparable criteria, honest limits, dated facts) and claim nothing we did not do.
