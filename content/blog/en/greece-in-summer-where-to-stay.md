# Greece in summer: where to stay for a single-base week

For Greece in summer, choose one base and stay there. Nafplio on the mainland suits a compact old town with a hillside fortress above it. Chania on Crete suits a harbour town with a larger area to wander. Athens suits you if you want a major city with its own pace. A week in one place is a complete trip, and it avoids the transfers that quietly take over island-hopping plans.

The useful skill in a Greek summer is not finding more places. It is arranging each day around the heat, so that exposed visits happen when you can bear them and the rest of the day stays easy. This guide is built on that idea.

Everything below is a proposed framework, not a tested tour or a report on current conditions. Opening hours, access to sites and transport change, so confirm them with the relevant operators for your own dates.

## Nafplio, Chania or Athens: choose a base by the evening you want

Daytime plans tend to look alike. The difference between bases is what you do when the sun goes down and the streets cool.

- **Nafplio, for a compact old town.** Its streets are small enough to walk without a plan, and the fortress above gives you one strenuous visit to schedule carefully. Evenings are spent in the old town.
- **Chania, for a harbour setting.** The old harbour is the centre of gravity. A larger town surrounds it, which gives you more choice but also more of a crowd in the evening.
- **Athens, for a city week.** You get museums, neighbourhoods and the sense of a working capital. It is a different kind of holiday from the other two, and it asks for different heat management.

Then ask how much you value variety. Nafplio is smaller and gives fewer new things after a few days. Chania gives more places to go but a more crowded waterfront. Athens gives the most to do but the least sense of retreat.

Do not assume any of them will be quiet in summer. Choose by what you want your day to look like, and accept that popular places are busy at popular times.

## How to plan a day around the heat

In summer, the day has two usable windows: early and late. The middle is for rest, food and indoors.

A simple structure works for any base. Put the most demanding or exposed visit first thing, finish it before the sun is high and plan nothing else that requires walking until the evening. Lunch should be long and shaded. The afternoon is for a swim if one is possible and safe, a nap or an indoor visit. The evening is for the waterfront, a slow dinner and a walk.

Check the forecast close to each day, not only before you leave. Heat can vary from one day to the next, and a sunny forecast does not tell you how exposed a path is. If you are not sure about a long outdoor visit, make it optional and have a shaded alternative ready.

Carry water, sun protection and a hat. Decide in advance when you will turn back. Turning back is part of the plan, not a failure.

## A Nafplio week: old town, fortress and one early start

Nafplio works best when you treat the fortress as a single, deliberate visit. The Palamidi castle sits above the town and involves a climb, so the sensible time for it is early. Check the way you intend to reach it, the opening arrangements and whether access varies on your day, and decide beforehand what you will do if the heat arrives sooner than expected.

**Proposed framework for a Nafplio day.**

- **Early morning:** the fortress visit, or the old town streets if you prefer an easy start.
- **Late morning:** an unhurried coffee and a walk to the waterfront.
- **Midday:** a long lunch in the shade and time back at your accommodation.
- **Late afternoon:** the old town again, this time with no plan except to wander.
- **Evening:** dinner in the old town, then a short walk.

On other days, repeat the pattern with a different anchor: a museum, a neighbourhood, a short outing. Do not try to see everything within reach of Nafplio. A single-base week works because you give up the idea of covering the region.

If you are thinking of day trips from Nafplio, test them against the heat. A day that includes an hour of driving and a midday visit to an exposed site is harder than it looks. Make each outing early or leave it out.

## A Chania week: the old harbour and a slower second half

Chania's old harbour is the natural centre, and a first day can be as simple as walking around it, finding a place to sit and watching the light change. After that, give each day a different purpose.

**Proposed framework for a Chania day.** Choose a morning activity that works before the heat builds: a walk through the old town, a market visit or an early swim if conditions allow. Rest in the middle of the day. In the late afternoon, return to the harbour area and eat there or nearby, then walk back slowly. Check the sea conditions and any beach arrangements on the day, instead of assuming a safe swim.

On a second or third day, choose something with a defined end: a museum, a short coastal outing arranged with an operator you have checked, or a day with nothing planned at all. Crete is large, and driving long distances in summer heat can use up the day without giving much back. A base like Chania works best when you keep your outings close and let the town fill the rest.

Evenings are busier here than in Nafplio. If you prefer a calmer dinner, eat a little earlier or choose a street away from the waterfront.

## Athens as a city option

Athens works differently. You are not looking for a retreat but for a city with enough variety to fill a week. The heat shapes the pattern: museums and indoor visits in the middle of the day, outdoor sights early or late and long evenings.

**Proposed framework for an Athens day.** Choose one outdoor visit for early morning and check its opening hours and access, since hours can vary with the season. Move to an indoor stop in the hottest part of the day. Return to a neighbourhood walk when the light softens. Keep dinner local to where you finish.

Athens suits a shorter stay or a week for travellers who like the energy of a big city. If you want a slower week, one of the other bases will suit you better. You can also treat Athens as the start or end of a trip to Nafplio, but check transfer arrangements near your dates and allow time for luggage rather than assuming a quick change of base.

## Why a single base is not a compromise

Island hopping is often presented as the natural way to see Greece. It can be, but each move costs time. You pack, travel, wait, unpack and then orient yourself again. In summer, the transfers are also exposed to heat and queues.

A single base lets you learn one place. You find a favourite cafe, notice when the streets are quieter and have time to turn back from a plan that is not working. If, after a few days, you really want somewhere else, check current transport and add a short change of base, but only then.

If you want some variety without moving, vary the type of day: one exposed visit, one museum day, one water day if conditions allow, one with nothing planned.

## Departure checklist for Greece in summer

- Opening hours and access for any fortress, archaeological or exposed visit, checked close to travel.
- A heat plan for each day: early start, midday pause, late outing.
- Water, sun protection, a hat and shoes suited to rough ground.
- Accommodation checked for shade, cooling and the walk from the nearest street.
- Any sea activity checked with the operator or local information on the day.
- Offline copies of addresses and tickets.
- A buffer day with nothing booked, in case heat or a delay changes the plan.

## Limits of this guide

It does not promise that any site will be open, that the sea will be calm or that a place will be quiet. It does not cover other islands or regions, and it does not replace current transport information. If you are travelling from within Greece, you may already know how heat shapes a day, and your own habits should win over this outline.
