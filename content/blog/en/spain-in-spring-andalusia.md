# Spain in spring: Andalusia itinerary choices

For Spain in spring, the first Andalusia decision is whether you want one city or three. A long weekend works best in Seville alone, with the Real Alcázar as the fixed visit. A week can stretch to Seville, Córdoba and Granada, with the Mezquita-Catedral and the Alhambra as the other two anchors. In both cases, book or confirm the fixed visits first and keep everything around them loose.

Andalusia in spring is popular for good reasons, but popular also means the main sights need attention before you arrive. This guide does not give a day-by-day script. It gives a way to decide how many cities you can enjoy, then shows how to protect time around the big visits.

The outlines below are proposed frameworks, not tested tours or current condition reports. Confirm opening days, entry arrangements and any local events with the operators or organisers for your own dates.

## One city or three: the anchor-first decision

Start by naming one anchor for each city you are considering.

- **Seville:** the Real Alcázar.
- **Córdoba:** the Mezquita-Catedral.
- **Granada:** the Alhambra.

Each anchor deserves a real block of time and may need an entry arrangement. Once you have written the anchors down, count them. One anchor is a long weekend. Two or three is a week or more. If the number of days does not match the number of anchors plus travel, drop one city rather than shorten all of them.

Next, ask what you want from the time around the anchors. A neighbourhood walk, a long lunch, an afternoon with no plan? These are what make a trip feel like a place rather than a checklist. If you cannot picture them, the itinerary is probably too full.

Finally, ask whether you mind packing and unpacking. Three bases means two moves. Some travellers prefer to sleep in Seville and visit Córdoba on a day out; others prefer a night in each. Both are legitimate. What matters is that you choose knowingly and that the day of the move is treated as a half day.

## A Seville long weekend built around the Real Alcázar

For three or four days, Seville is enough. Give the Real Alcázar a morning, since it rewards a slow visit. Check the entry arrangements and opening days first, and decide before you go whether you prefer an earlier slot or a later one.

**Proposed framework for a Seville weekend.**

- **Day one:** arrive, drop your bags and walk the neighbourhood around your accommodation without a list.
- **Day two:** the Real Alcázar in the morning, a long lunch and a flexible afternoon. Leave the cathedral area or a riverside walk as options, not obligations.
- **Day three:** a neighbourhood day. Choose a district you have not seen, keep a market or a museum in reserve and finish with an unscheduled evening.
- **Day four, if you have it:** a slow departure or a short walk back to a favourite spot.

Do not assume that patio events or festival activity will coincide with your dates. If a particular local event matters to you, check the organiser's information for your year, and treat anything else as a bonus. The main point is that a Seville weekend does not need to depend on one.

## A proposed Seville, Córdoba and Granada week

A week can be sensible if you accept that each city gets one anchor day and one flexible day. This is a proposed framework, not a promise that every stage will fit your travel arrangements.

- **Days one to three, Seville:** the Real Alcázar, a neighbourhood day and a rest day with no fixed plan.
- **Day four, Córdoba:** either a day out from Seville or a night there. The Mezquita-Catedral is the anchor; the old streets around it fill the rest. If you stay overnight, an evening walk after the main visitors have left can be the best part.
- **Days five to seven, Granada:** the Alhambra as the fixed visit, then a flexible day in the surrounding neighbourhoods and a departure day.

**A Córdoba day, if you add it.** Córdoba is smaller than Seville, so one anchor is enough. Visit the Mezquita-Catedral first and check the current entry arrangements before you choose your hours, since the visit can include a queue or a timed slot. Afterwards, let the streets around it set the pace: a slow walk, a longer lunch and a short return to the station or your room. The mistake to avoid is adding a second major sight because the day feels short. A small city is easier to enjoy when you leave part of it unseen, and the unseen part gives you a reason to come back.

Check how you will move between cities and how long the transfer actually takes on your chosen day. Leave room for luggage and for finding your accommodation. If you are carrying everything on foot, give the move more time than a map suggests.

If a week feels crowded, take Córdoba out. A Seville and Granada pairing with a long gap between them can feel less rushed than three cities in seven days. You can add Córdoba on another trip, and it will be better for having its own time.

## Granada and the Alhambra: one fixed visit, one flexible day

Granada needs particular care because the Alhambra is likely to be the main reason you are going. Check the official booking process, entry arrangements and any rules about timed access before you commit to dates. Do not rely on turning up and hoping.

**Proposed framework for a Granada day.** Put the Alhambra at the centre. Decide whether your slot is early or later and plan the rest around it: a short walk before, an unhurried lunch after and nothing that needs you to run back across the city. Wear shoes you can walk in for a long time, because the site is large and includes a good deal of walking outdoors, which means weather matters.

On a second Granada day, choose the surrounding neighbourhood. An area of narrow streets and viewpoints, a market or a long cafe stop does not need a ticket. This is where the trip begins to feel like Granada rather than a visit to one monument. If rain arrives, move your indoor option forward and keep walking when it eases.

## Spring weather: plan for variation, not a typical day

Spring in Andalusia varies between years and even between days. You might have pleasant walking weather, a warm afternoon or a cold, wet spell, and the same week can bring more than one. Do not plan around a remembered season or a photograph from another year.

Instead, pack so you can adapt: layers, rain protection, comfortable shoes and something for the sun. Choose indoor and outdoor options for each day and let the forecast near your date choose between them. A visit with a fixed entry slot is the exception, so give those days the most thought.

If heat is a concern, put walking and outdoor visits in the cooler parts of the day and keep the middle of the day for a long lunch or indoor time. That structure works in warm weather and costs nothing in cool weather.

## Alternatives if the full route feels too much

- **Seville only, with a slow second half.** Fewer sights and more neighbourhood time.
- **Granada only.** A short break built around the Alhambra and its surroundings.
- **Córdoba and Granada, skipping Seville.** Fewer moves if those two anchors matter most to you.
- **Seville and Córdoba only.** A shorter trip with one day out and a lighter schedule.

Choose by the anchors you would be disappointed to miss, not by the number of cities you can claim. Fewer places with room to breathe usually give a better record of the trip.

## A departure checklist for Andalusia in spring

- Entry arrangements confirmed for the Real Alcázar, Mezquita-Catedral and Alhambra, where relevant to your plan.
- Accommodation checked for how you reach it on foot, including steps and narrow streets.
- Transfers between cities confirmed close to travel, with luggage in mind.
- Layers, rain protection, sun protection and shoes for long walks.
- Offline copies of tickets and addresses, kept apart from your notes.
- One flexible block each day with nothing booked.
- A note of the day you are moving between cities, treated as a half day.

## What this guide cannot decide

It cannot tell you the weather, confirm entry availability or say which days will be busy. It also does not cover the wider region, such as other cities or the coast. If you are travelling from elsewhere in Spain, your arrival and departure will differ from a traveller flying in, so adjust the framework to your own journey.
