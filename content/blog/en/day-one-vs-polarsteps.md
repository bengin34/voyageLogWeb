# Day One vs Polarsteps: journal or tracker for your trip?

Day One vs Polarsteps comes down to one question: do you want to write about the trip, or have the trip recorded for you? Day One is a daily journal, and travel is one of many things people put in it. Polarsteps is a travel tracker: it draws your route automatically and lets friends follow along. We make VoyageLog, a third kind of app, so read the sections about it with that in mind. The rest of this guide sticks to what the two main apps do and when each one is the right pick.

## What Day One is good at

Day One is a journaling app first. Each entry can carry photos, a location and the weather, and the writing experience is the point. If you already write most evenings, your travel entries will feel natural here, because the habit exists before the trip does.

The free Basic plan gives you unlimited entries and journals, end-to-end encryption and one photo per entry. The paid plans add more photos and syncing across devices. Day One also says your data is stored on your device, and that signing in with a free account backs it up.

The limit for travel is structure. A trip in Day One is a set of entries you organize yourself, with tags or separate journals. There is no list of places, no rating, and no map of where you have been. That is fine for a writer. It is less fine on day nine in Lisbon, when you cannot remember which tasca had the good grilled sardines.

## What Polarsteps is good at

Polarsteps is the app most people picture when they hear "travel tracker". You create an account, start a trip, and it records your route in the background. You add steps along the way with photos, videos and stories. Friends and family can follow the trip, and you choose who sees it.

Polarsteps says it keeps tracking even when you are off the grid, which matters on a long drive through patchy coverage. It also says it is ad-free. The core app is free, and there is an optional paid tier, Polarsteps Plus, that adds things like 3D maps and extra map styles. We are not quoting Plus prices, because they vary by country.

The limit is the same as for every tracker. The entry is a step on a map, not a rated place in a list. And a trip is shared by default with the people you pick, so it is not the app you open for a private record. ## Day One travel journal vs a route tracker: how to choose

Think about what you will open in five years.

- **A page of writing.** You want the feeling of the day: the argument at the ferry, the smell of the market in Marrakech. Day One.
- **A line on a map.** You want to show your parents how you got from Hanoi to Hue. Polarsteps.
- **Both.** This is common, and it works. Polarsteps does the effortless part in the background. Day One holds the two minutes of writing you do at night.

If you are still deciding what shape your notes should take, the guide on [how to keep a travel journal](/en/blog/how-to-keep-a-travel-journal/) walks through a habit that takes about a minute per entry.

## Three trips, three answers

Concrete cases help more than feature lists.

**Two weeks on a road trip through Iceland, with parents following.** Polarsteps. Start it at Keflavik, add a photo at each stop, and the line moves across the map without you messaging anyone. Day One would give them nothing to follow.

**A month alone in Japan, where you want the thoughts, not the route.** Day One. A page each night about the konbini breakfast and the wrong train, with a photo or two. In a year you will reread the writing, not the map.

**A long weekend each in Prague, Vienna and Budapest, where you mostly want the good places.** Neither app is shaped for this. You would be tagging café entries by hand in Day One, or scrolling a route in Polarsteps that tells you nothing about which place was worth it. ## Polarsteps offline vs Day One offline

Both apps cope with a bad signal, in different ways.

Day One keeps entries on the device, so writing a page in a cabin with no coverage is no problem. Syncing to other devices needs a connection and a paid plan.

Polarsteps keeps tracking without a connection and uploads your locations later. That is helpful, but it means friends do not see a live line from a remote area. If you want followers to see you arrive, plan for a moment with signal.

## What neither app does for you

Both apps leave a gap that shows up after the trip, not during it.

Day One gives you a long scroll of entries. Finding "that restaurant in Kyoto" means remembering which night you wrote about it, or having tagged it at the time. Polarsteps gives you a route and a set of steps. It shows where you were, but a step is a story, not a verdict, so "would I go back?" is not a question the app can answer.

Neither one asks you to rate anything, and neither separates a place you visited from a moment that happened to you. That separation sounds small. In practice it is what makes a record searchable: a place is something you can return to, a moment is something you remember. We wrote about that split in more detail in our notes on [places and moments](/en/blog/places-vs-moments-travel-notes/).

## Day One vs Polarsteps privacy

This is where they differ most.

Polarsteps is built for sharing. You decide who can see a trip, but the default idea is that someone follows it, and you need an account to create trips at all. Day One is private by design: entries are end-to-end encrypted, and you can use it on one device without signing in. If you never sign in, nothing leaves the phone, but you also get no backup except the ones you make yourself.
