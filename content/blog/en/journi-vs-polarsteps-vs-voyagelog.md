# Journi vs Polarsteps vs VoyageLog: which one fits your trip?

Journi vs Polarsteps is not really a contest, because the two apps do different jobs. Polarsteps records your route automatically and turns it into a map you share with friends and family. Journi is a blog-style journal built from photos and text, and its main product is a printed photo book. VoyageLog is a third thing: a private journal of places you rate and moments you note, with no account and no route tracking. We make VoyageLog, so read what we say about it with that in mind. Below, all three side by side, and plainly when to pick each one.

## Three apps, three jobs

Most "travel journal app" comparisons line apps up as if they were rivals. They are not. Ask what you want at the end of the trip.

- You want a **map of the route** your friends followed while you were away. That is Polarsteps.
- You want a **printed book** on the shelf. Journi is built around that.
- You want a **private record** of what was good, what was not, and what happened, that you can search in five years. That is VoyageLog.

Some people want two of these. That is fine. The rest of this guide shows where each app stops, so you can combine them without surprises. If you want the wider field first, see our list of the [best travel journal apps](/en/blog/best-travel-journal-apps/).

## Polarsteps in one section

Polarsteps describes itself as an app that captures your route automatically. It runs in the background, plots your path, and lets you add photos, videos and stories as steps along it. Trips can be visible to only you, to your followers or to everyone. Polarsteps says it shows no ads, and that a full day of tracking uses about 4% of the battery. That is the company's own figure, so treat it as a typical case, not a promise.

### What Polarsteps does well

- **Automatic tracking.** You do not have to remember anything. The route draws itself.
- **Offline tracking.** Polarsteps says tracking keeps going without a connection. Locations are not synced in real time, to save battery, and the high-accuracy mode, which is the default, uses more battery than the balanced one.
- **A trip your family can follow.** This is the reason many people install it.
- **A hardback book.** Polarsteps offers to turn a trip into a hardback travel book.

### Where it stops

Polarsteps is a route-first app. The entry is a step on a map, not a rated place in a list. If you want to ask "which cafés did I give a 5?", the app is not shaped for it. There is also an optional paid tier, Polarsteps Plus. The core app stays free, including tracking, unlimited photo and video uploads and travel stats, while Plus adds 3D maps, more map styles and step statistics. We are not quoting Plus prices, because they vary by country. If you are looking at it for the first time, our guide to [Polarsteps alternatives](/en/blog/polarsteps-alternatives/) covers what people switch to and why.

## Journi in one section

Journi is a travel journal app that groups your photos by time and location and lets you build a story from photos, text, maps, stamps, weather and flight details. The journal app is now called Journi Blog, and photo books are made in a separate Journi Print app. You can invite others to follow or contribute. Journi builds maps, location pins and country stamps from the location data in your photos, so the map comes from the pictures you take.

### What Journi does well

- **Looks good as a story.** It is a blog-style layout, with a timeline and a map.
- **Print products.** Photo books in hardcover, softcover and layflat, in square, landscape and portrait formats, plus prints and calendars. Books start at 25 pages and go up to 349 for hardcover and softcover, or 103 for layflat.
- **Backups.** Premium members can back up to Dropbox or Google Drive.
- **Offline use.** Journi lists offline use, without internet, among its free features.

### Where it stops

You can start a journal and design a photo book without registering, but ordering, commenting, following and sharing need an account. Offline use is free; cloud saving, backup, weather and flight details and the full sticker set are Premium. Book prices depend on size, page count and cover, with shipping on top, so we will not quote a single price. Printing is where Journi makes its money, and the journal feeds that. If you never want a book, you are using a lot of app you do not need.

## What each app is not

This is where honesty saves you money.

- **Polarsteps is not a place-rating journal, and it is not private by default.** New trips are visible to your followers unless you change the setting. It is built for sharing a route.
- **Journi is built from your photos.** Its maps come from where your pictures were taken, so a stretch with no photos leaves a gap.
- **VoyageLog does not track your route.** If you want that, use Polarsteps. VoyageLog also has no feed and no sharing network: you can share a card or a PDF, but nothing is posted anywhere.

## Polarsteps vs Journi: which one for which trip

A few concrete cases.

**A two-week road trip with family following along.** Polarsteps. Switch it on at the airport, add a photo here and there, and your parents see the line move across the map. Nobody needs to message you.

**A honeymoon you want in a book.** Journi. You will take hundreds of photos in Amalfi and want eighty of them in a layout you can hold. Journi's job is exactly this.

**A city break in Prague, Vienna and Budapest where you want to remember the good places.** VoyageLog. Five entries a day, each with a rating and a line. A year later you filter by 5 stars and tag "coffee" and you have a list to send a friend.

**A long trip with a lot of border crossings.** Polarsteps for the line, VoyageLog for the judgments. They do not conflict. One is automatic, the other takes one minute per entry. Our guide on [how to keep a travel journal](/en/blog/how-to-keep-a-travel-journal/) explains that one-minute habit.

## Choose Polarsteps if

- You want the route drawn without any effort.
- People at home want to follow along.
- You are fine with a background app using some battery.
- You may want a hardback book of the trip later.

## Choose Journi if

- The printed photo book is the goal and the journal is how you get there.
- You like writing stories in a blog-style layout.
- You are happy to register an account when you order.
- You do not need live route tracking.

## Choose VoyageLog if

- You want a private journal with no account and no server. Everything stays on the phone.
- You think in places and moments: a café in Lisbon you would go back to (a place, rated 1 to 5) and the evening you got caught in the rain on the way down (a moment, unrated).
- You want to find things later: search across titles, notes and tags, filter by type or rating.
- You want copies you own: a free data backup with a reminder every ten trips, PDF and image cards, and JSON, Excel or ZIP with media on Pro.
- You are fine without route tracking and without a printed book.

VoyageLog also has Insights (trips, countries, days away, average rating), a month-by-month Year in Travel, and city lists, including ready-made ones for Christmas markets and cherry blossom season.
