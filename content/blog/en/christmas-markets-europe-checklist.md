# Christmas markets in Europe: a 25-city checklist to tick off

There are far more good Christmas markets in Europe than anyone can see in one winter, which is the right way to think about them: not as a single trip but as a list you work through over years. Below are 25 cities, grouped by region, with what makes each market distinct, the specific thing to look for and the 2026 dates where the organizers had published them when we checked. Traditional German and Alsatian markets usually run from the second half of November to 23 or 24 December; many in capital cities stay open to New Year or 6 January. Dates shift every year, so confirm before booking.

## Germany and Austria

The heartland. German markets are the template everyone else copies: wooden stalls, a tall tree, a stage for choirs, bratwurst, and a mug of Glühwein you pay a deposit on and either return or keep. Austrian markets add the Punsch, hotter and sweeter, and the setting of imperial squares.

### 1. Nuremberg

The Christkindlesmarkt is the one people mean when they say German Christmas market. It fills the Hauptmarkt under the Frauenkirche, where the Christkind opens the market from the church balcony, and it keeps deliberately to traditional goods. Look for the Nuremberg Lebkuchen, the soft gingerbread the city has baked for centuries, and the tiny Zwetschgenmännle, figures made of dried prunes. Eat the small Nuremberg sausages, three in a bun. Dates 2026: 27 November to 24 December, closing at 2pm on Christmas Eve.

### 2. Dresden

The Striezelmarkt calls itself Germany's oldest, dating to 1434, and it is built around Stollen, the dense fruit bread. The Stollenfest, on 5 December in 2026, celebrates it with a giant Stollen. Look for the big Christmas pyramid, the carved Erzgebirge wooden figures, and the Pflaumentoffel, little figures made from dried prunes. Dates 2026: 25 November to 24 December.

### 3. Cologne

Cologne has several markets, and the one that matters is at the foot of the cathedral on Roncalliplatz, where the stalls sit under the two black spires. The others are worth a wander too: Heinzels Wintermärchen in the old town by the Rhine, with an ice rink on the Heumarkt, and a harbour market by the Chocolate Museum. Look for Reibekuchen, fried potato cakes, and for the view of the cathedral lit up from across the market. Dates 2026: the cathedral market runs 16 November to 23 December (closed 22 November); the Heumarkt and the harbour market stay open into January.

### 4. Munich

The market on Marienplatz sits under the Rathaus, with the Glockenspiel in the tower above. It is more urban and less pretty than Nuremberg, and the Bavarian specialities are worth it: Schmalznudeln (fried dough) and roasted almonds. Look for the Kripperlmarkt next door, by Alter Peter and the Rindermarkt, a market devoted entirely to nativity figures. Dates 2026: 20 November to 24 December (closed 22 November). From this year it opens on the Friday before Totensonntag, earlier than it used to.

### 5. Berlin

Berlin has dozens of markets, which makes it a city where you can tick off several in one day. The WeihnachtsZauber, back on the Gendarmenmarkt, is the elegant one, with a €2 entry fee (free for under-12s and on weekday lunchtimes outside the holidays). Charlottenburg Palace has the prettiest setting and is free. The one at Alexanderplatz is big and loud. Dates 2026: Gendarmenmarkt 23 November to 31 December, Charlottenburg 23 November to 27 December (closed 24 December), Alexanderplatz 23 November to 26 December.

### 6. Rothenburg ob der Tauber

A small walled town that looks like a Christmas card and leans into it. The Reiterlesmarkt is modest in size and strongest in setting, with the half-timbered houses and the town walls as backdrop. Look for Schneeballen, pastry balls dusted in sugar, and for the year-round Christmas shop on Herrngasse near the square. Dates 2026: 20 November to 23 December.

### 7. Vienna

Vienna does not have a Christmas market so much as a season of them. The big one in front of the Rathaus is the postcard, with a skating rink alongside it. The one in front of Schönbrunn Palace is calmer and more handsome, and it opens first and closes last. The small one at Spittelberg, in narrow Biedermeier lanes, is the one Viennese people actually like. Look for Punsch in a dozen flavours, Kaiserschmarrn, and the way the city's own cafés become part of the circuit. Dates 2026: Rathausplatz 13 November to 26 December, Spittelberg 13 November to 23 December, Schönbrunn 6 November to 6 January.

### 8. Salzburg

The Christkindlmarkt sits on Domplatz and Residenzplatz under the cathedral and the fortress, and the Alps are visible at the end of the streets. It is one of the oldest and it feels it. Look for the Krampus and Perchten runs, when men in terrifying horned costumes parade through (the market publishes the dates), and for the choirs singing in front of the cathedral. Dates 2026: 19 November to 1 January.

### 9. Innsbruck

Innsbruck's old-town market sits under the Golden Roof with the Nordkette mountains directly above, so close they look painted on. There is a second market on Marktplatz, a weekend market on the Bergisel hill and another up at Hungerburg. Look for the fairy-tale figures and the giants in the old-town lanes, and for Kiachl, fried dough served sweet or with sauerkraut. Dates 2026: the old town and Marktplatz from mid November to 23 December, Bergisel Friday to Sunday until 20 December, Hungerburg until 6 January.

## Central Europe

Prague, Budapest, Kraków and the Baltic capitals have markets that are younger as tourist destinations but set in squares that no German city can match.

### 10. Prague

The market on Old Town Square is the one you have seen in photos: a tall tree, the Týn church spires behind, and the astronomical clock a few steps away. Wenceslas Square has a second market a short walk off. Look for trdelník, the spiral pastry roasted over coals, for Czech mead, and for the square at 8am with light snow and nobody there. Dates 2026: 28 November to 6 January.

### 11. Budapest

Budapest has two main markets. The one on Vörösmarty Square is the established one, with craft stalls and plenty of food. The one in front of St. Stephen's Basilica is the atmospheric one, with light shows on the basilica. Look for kürtőskalács (the chimney cake), lángos, and the Hungarian mulled wine, which is often better than the German sort. Dates 2026: the Basilica market runs 13 November to 1 January; check Vörösmarty Square's dates before you book.

### 12. Kraków

The market fills the enormous Main Square, one of the largest medieval squares in Europe, with the Cloth Hall in the middle and St. Mary's trumpet call on the hour. Look for oscypek, smoked sheep's cheese grilled and served with cranberry jam, and for the szopki, Kraków's glittering nativity scenes, which have their own competition each December. Dates 2026: not yet confirmed when we checked, so look at the city's own site before booking.

### 13. Tallinn

A compact market on Town Hall Square, which claims the world's first public Christmas tree and has the medieval walls and towers right behind. It is small, cold and pretty. Look for black pudding with sauerkraut, the Estonian Christmas staple, and for the glögi, the Nordic-style mulled wine with almonds and raisins in the cup. Dates 2026: 20 November to 6 January.

### 14. Zagreb

Zagreb's Advent spreads across the whole centre and won fans by being more fun than solemn: ice skating on King Tomislav Square, live music and the upper town lit up. Look for fritule, small doughnuts, and for the view from the Strossmayer promenade. Dates 2026: 28 November to 7 January.

### 15. Bratislava

A short train or boat from Vienna and much less visited. The market fills the Main Square and Hviezdoslav Square in the old town, with a strong food emphasis. Look for lokše, potato flatbreads with goose fat or poppy seeds, and for cigánska, a pork cutlet in a bun that is the local Christmas-market staple. Dates 2026: 26 November to 3 January, closed 24 and 25 December.

## The Nordics

Nordic markets are smaller, darker and shorter, with daylight ending mid-afternoon and candles everywhere. Some run only on certain days.

### 16. Copenhagen

Tivoli Gardens turns into a Christmas market with the rides running, and the whole park is lit. You need a Tivoli entry ticket. Smaller markets elsewhere in the city are free to walk into. Look for æbleskiver, round pancake puffs with jam and sugar, and for gløgg. Dates 2026: Tivoli runs 13 November to 3 January.

### 17. Stockholm

The market in Stortorget, the main square of Gamla Stan, is small and old, with red stalls against the painted houses. Skansen, the open-air museum, holds its own Christmas market with traditional crafts. Look for saffron buns, pepparkakor, and the Lucia processions around 13 December. Dates: Stortorget had not published 2026 dates when we checked (in 2025 it ran from 22 November to 23 December); Skansen's market starts on 27 November.

### 18. Gothenburg

Liseberg amusement park turns into a Christmas market, with lights strung through the park and stalls between the rides. Look for the Swedish Christmas food and for the julbord, the Christmas table buffets served around the city. Dates 2026: not yet confirmed when we checked, so look at Liseberg's own site before booking.

## Western Europe

### 19. Strasbourg

Strasbourg calls itself the capital of Christmas and has a reasonable claim. The Christkindelsmärik on Place Broglie has run since 1570, and markets spread across several squares under the cathedral and through the Petite France quarter. Look for bredele, the small Alsatian Christmas biscuits in dozens of shapes, for vin chaud, and for the great tree on Place Kléber. Dates 2026: 27 November to 24 December.

### 20. Colmar

An hour from Strasbourg and even prettier, with six small markets in the half-timbered old town and along the canals of Little Venice. It is small enough to see in an afternoon and crowded on weekends. Look for the half-timbered houses lit after dark and for the Alsatian food. Dates 2026: 23 November to 29 December.

### 21. Brussels

The winter market, Plaisirs d'Hiver in French and Winterpret in Dutch, runs from the Bourse through Sainte-Catherine and the old fish market, while the Grand Place gets a tall tree and a light show projected onto its facades. Look for the light show specifically, and for the stalls of speculoos biscuits and Belgian waffles. Dates 2026: 27 November to 3 January.

### 22. Basel

Basel's Christmas market splits between Barfüsserplatz and Münsterplatz in the old town. It is handsome and orderly. Look for Basler Läckerli, the city's spiced honey biscuit, and for raclette scraped onto bread at the stalls. Dates 2026: not yet confirmed when we checked, so look at Basel's tourism site before booking.

### 23. Edinburgh

East Princes Street Gardens fill with a market, a big wheel and fairground rides below the castle. It is more fairground than craft market, but the setting under the castle at 4pm, with the light going, is hard to beat. Look for the view from the wheel, and for mulled cider rather than wine. Dates 2026: not yet confirmed when we checked, so look at Edinburgh's Christmas site before booking.

## Elsewhere

### 24. Montbéliard, France

Included because it is the kind of market regulars talk about when the famous ones get too crowded. A small town in eastern France near the Swiss border, with a market that lights the whole centre and invites a guest of honour: Switzerland in 2026. Look for the Lumières de Noël and for the regional food, including the Montbéliard sausage. Dates 2026: 21 November to 24 December.

### 25. Valkenburg, Netherlands

Valkenburg's markets are underground, in the old marl caves beneath the town, with stalls lining passages cut into the rock. There are two, in the Gemeentegrot (Municipal Cave) and the Fluweelengrot (Velvet Cave), and the town above has a lit walk. Look for the cool, damp air of the caves, a strange contrast with the stalls of Glühwein, and for the Dutch oliebollen, deep-fried dough balls sold everywhere. Dates 2026: both cave markets run 13 November to 30 December.

## How to use this as a checklist

Twenty-five cities is a decade of winters for most people, and that is the point. Treat it as a list to tick off rather than a plan to complete.

- **Group by trip.** Nuremberg, Rothenburg and Munich are one train trip. Vienna, Bratislava and Budapest are another. Strasbourg and Colmar are a weekend. Copenhagen and Gothenburg connect by train in a few hours.
- **One new region each year.** It keeps the markets from blurring into each other.
- **Go midweek where you can.** Saturdays in Colmar and Rothenburg are crushes. Tuesdays are not.
- **Rate each market.** A rating out of five after each visit turns a list into an opinion, and an opinion is what friends ask for.

## How to journal a market visit

A Christmas market is a good test of a travel journal, because the visits are short, sensory and almost identical to each other on paper. Capture these six things and they stay distinct.

1. **Save the market as a place the moment you arrive.** Title it with the square, not just the city: Old Town Square, Rathausplatz, Striezelmarkt. Vienna alone will give you five entries.
2. **Rate it before you leave.** Do not wait until the hotel. Standing at the edge of the square with the mug in your hand, you know whether this was a 3 or a 5.
3. **Write one line about the specific thing.** Not the atmosphere, which is the same everywhere. The oscypek with cranberry. The brass band. The queue for the Stollen. The light show on the Grand Place.
4. **Photograph the mug.** Most big markets have their own, often with the year on it, and the collection of mug photos across years is a better souvenir than the mugs.
5. **Tag it.** One tag, christmas-market, across every visit, means that in five years one search returns all of them in order.
6. **Add the moments separately.** The snow starting in Salzburg, the Krampus run, the choir on the cathedral steps. These are moments, not places, and they are the entries you will reread.
