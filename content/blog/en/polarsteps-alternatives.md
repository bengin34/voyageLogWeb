# Polarsteps alternatives: 6 apps for private, manual trips

Most people looking for a Polarsteps alternative like the app fine. They just want something it is not built to be: a private journal rather than a shared route map, with no account, no background tracking and no audience. Below: why people switch, then six alternatives, from private journals to other trackers that handle a few things differently. We make VoyageLog, the first app on the list, so read its entry with that in mind.

## Why people look for a Polarsteps alternative

Polarsteps is good at its job. The Travel Tracker draws a route while you move, steps with photos hang off that route, and at the end the trip looks like a story your friends can scroll. The reasons people leave are almost never bugs. They are mismatches between what the app is for and what the traveller wanted.

### They want the trip to be private

Polarsteps lets you keep a trip private or share it by link, but the product is shaped around being followed. The profile, the followers, the comments, the book you can order to show others. New trips are visible to followers unless you change the setting. If nobody is going to watch, most of that is noise, and a surprising number of people would rather the trip simply did not exist on a server at all.

### They do not want an account

You cannot create a trip in Polarsteps without signing up with Google, Apple or an email address. That is normal for a cloud app, and it is a dealbreaker for a specific kind of person: the one who treats a travel diary like a paper notebook. A notebook does not ask for your email.

### Battery and background location

The tracker is the point of Polarsteps, and it is efficient for what it does: Polarsteps puts a full day of tracking at about 4% of the battery, and it does not sync locations in real time. Still, it is a location-tracking app that runs in the background for weeks. On an older phone, or for a traveller who already runs maps and a camera all day, the extra drain is real, and some people are uncomfortable with any app holding always-on location permission for that long.

### They want to write, not be tracked

This is the big one. A route map tells you where you walked. It does not tell you that the second espresso at the counter in Trastevere was better than the first, or that the Belvedere queue was fine on a weekday. People who care about those details want a form they fill in while standing there: a title, a rating, a line of notes, a photo. The automatic track becomes a distraction from the thing they actually wanted to keep.

### They want their data in a file

Polarsteps will send you a download of your data on request, but its export story is built around printed books. If you want a spreadsheet of every place you rated, or a backup file you can restore on a new phone yourself without any company in the middle, you want an app that treats export as a core feature.

If none of these apply to you, stay with Polarsteps. It is the best app for an automatic route map, and nothing below replaces that specifically.

## 1. VoyageLog

[VoyageLog](/) is the app we make, and it is the most direct answer to the complaints above, because it was built as the opposite of a tracker: a private, offline journal for remembering trips.

Each trip holds entries that are either places or moments. A place is somewhere you went, with a location you can open in Apple or Google Maps. A moment is something that happened. Both take photos, a note, tags and a rating out of five, and a new entry is a title and a tap; everything else is optional. Longer trips split by city, each with its own dates and entries. Around that you get a timeline of all trips, a trip map built from the places you saved, search with filters, Year in Travel, best-of lists you rank yourself, city lists (including ready-made ones like Christmas markets), and badges.

**Pros**

- No account. Open the app and start the first trip.
- Works entirely offline. Data is stored only on the phone.
- No feed, no followers, no notifications about who viewed what.
- No background location tracking, so nothing drains the battery while the phone is in your pocket.
- A free data backup (trips, entries and tags) to iCloud Drive or Files, plus PDF or image cards for a trip or a city. Pro adds exports to JSON (full backup you can import on a new phone), Excel and ZIP with all media.
- Free tier includes the full journal with a limited number of trips. Pro is a yearly subscription or a one-time lifetime purchase.

**Cons**

- No automatic route. If you want a line drawn across the map while you walk, this is not it.
- No live sharing. You can send a trip, a city or a best-of list as a card or PDF, but nobody follows along in real time.
- No web version. It is a phone app for iPhone and Android.
- Moving to a new phone is manual: save a backup, restore it on the other device.

Best for: travellers who want a private diary of places and moments that will still be searchable years later, and who do not want any company holding their trips.

## 2. Day One

Day One is a daily journaling app rather than a travel app, which is precisely why it works as a Polarsteps alternative for some people. If you already write every evening, your trip entries live naturally alongside the rest of your life, with location and weather attached automatically to each entry.

**Pros**

- Best long-form writing experience of any app here.
- End-to-end encryption and biometric lock in the free tier.
- Usable on one device without an account.
- Rich formatting, templates and prompts if you like structure.

**Cons**

- Free tier allows one photo per entry and one device. Syncing needs a paid plan and an account.
- Not trip-shaped. You separate trips with tags or journals rather than a built-in trip structure.
- No ratings, best-of lists or trip statistics.

Best for: people who want a writing habit first and trips second.

## 3. FindPenguins

FindPenguins is the closest thing to Polarsteps itself: an account, a background tracker with automatic flight detection, posts called footprints that you can draft offline and upload later, and a community that can follow your trip. Listing it as an alternative is honest only for one group: people who like the Polarsteps model but want a different community, or specific features such as flight detection.

**Pros**

- Automatic tracking and flight detection, working offline.
- Entirely free, with unlimited trips and no paid tier.
- Active community if you want an audience.

**Cons**

- Account required and data lives in the cloud, same as Polarsteps.
- Background location permission, same battery and privacy questions.
- Built around followers and comments, which is noise if nobody is watching.

Best for: long-haul travellers who want the tracker model and either prefer this community or want flight detection.

## 4. Journi

Journi builds a travel blog from your posts and photo locations, groups memories by time and place, and points firmly at a printed photo book at the end. Its maps come from the location data in your photos, so they show where your pictures were taken.

**Pros**

- Lovely books, and the whole flow is designed around producing one.
- Groups photos automatically by time and location, so a day's uploads become a tidy entry.
- Shareable with family through the app.

**Cons**

- An account is needed to order, comment or share, and saving to the cloud is a Premium feature.
- Backup to Dropbox or Google Drive is Premium only.
- Fewer tools for the years after the trip: no ratings, no best-of lists.

Best for: a traveller who wants the printed book at the end.

## 5. Journey

Journey is a general diary with clients on Android, iOS, Mac, Windows and the web. Entries carry photos and location, and the app syncs through its own Journey Cloud Sync. It is not built for trips, but it can hold them, and with Day One it is one of two options here with desktop apps.

**Pros**

- Works on nearly every platform, including desktop and web.
- A free version you can keep using, with a paid membership for more.
- Clean, calm interface with photo and location support.

**Cons**

- Sync requires a Journey account, and the fuller features sit behind a paid membership.
- No trip structure, so you organize with tags and dates.
- No automatic map, no ratings, no trip statistics.

Best for: someone who switches between a laptop and a phone and wants one diary everywhere.

## 6. Google Maps Timeline

Timeline is included because many people already have it and wonder whether it replaces a tracker. It records the places you visited and the routes between them using your phone's location, and since the move to on-device storage it keeps that history on your phone with an optional encrypted backup to your Google account.

**Pros**

- You probably already have it, and it costs nothing.
- Good at answering where was I on a given day.
- Data now stays on the device rather than Google's servers.

**Cons**

- It is a location log, not a journal. There is no place to write what you thought.
- Tied to your Google account and to Google Maps' settings and defaults, including auto-delete periods.
- No photos, ratings, notes or trip structure.

Best for: filling in the gaps after a trip when you are trying to remember the name of a street. Not a journal on its own.

One last thing. Switching apps rarely rescues a journaling habit on its own. The traveller who keeps writing after a trip is the one who found a format small enough to repeat: a title, a rating, one sentence, done. If an alternative gives you that and removes the friction that was stopping you, it was worth the switch. If it just moves the same friction somewhere else, it was not.
